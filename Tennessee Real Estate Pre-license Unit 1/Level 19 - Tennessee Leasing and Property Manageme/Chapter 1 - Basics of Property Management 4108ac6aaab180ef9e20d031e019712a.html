Chapter 1 - Basics of Property Management

Property Management & The Role of the Property Manager

  • Property Management: The vocation of renting, leasing, maintaining, and operating another person's real property in exchange for compensation under a formal property management agreement.
  • Property Manager: An individual or firm hired by a property owner to handle marketing, tenant relations, rent collection, accounting, maintenance, and day-to-day operations.
  • Primary Objective: To preserve and enhance the property's value while realizing the highest possible return on the owner's investment (ROI).
  • When Owners Hire Property Managers: While hands-on owner-occupants of small multi-unit properties often self-manage, owners with large portfolios, limited time, or a lack of accounting and maintenance expertise rely on professional property managers.
  • The Three Main Types of Property Managers
    Manager TypeScope & DutiesEmployment & LicensingOn-Site Residency
    Individual Property ManagerManages multiple properties for one or more ownersOften licensed real estate brokers; either self-employed or part of a property management firmNot required to live on-site
    Individual Building ManagerOversees day-to-day operations of one larger propertyHired directly by a property owner or another property manager; not required to hold a real estate licenseNot required to live on-site
    Resident ManagerManages only residential propertiesEmployed by a real estate broker, managing agent, or property ownerRequired to live on-site (sometimes mandated by state law based on building size)
  • Four Broad Categories of Managed Properties
    • Property managers may specialize in or interact with four main asset classes, each requiring distinct operational, legal, and maintenance expertise:
      1. Residential Property: Single-family homes, duplexes, multi-family apartment complexes, condominiums, and townhomes.
      1. Commercial Property: Retail centers, shopping malls, office buildings, and mixed-use business spaces.
      1. Industrial Property: Warehouses, manufacturing plants, distribution centers, and research/flex facilities.
      1. Special-Purpose Property: Hotels/motels, nursing homes, self-storage facilities, schools, resorts, and recreational venues.
  • The Basic Duties & Responsibilities of Property Managers
    • Property managers oversee seven primary operational areas on behalf of the property owner:
      1. Marketing: Advertising vacancies, staging/showing units, and attracting qualified tenants.
      1. Tenant Relationships: Handling tenant communications, screening applicants, addressing complaints, and fostering retention.
      1. Rent Setting and Collection: Conducting market rent analyses, establishing competitive rates, and enforcing timely rent collection.
      1. Lease Agreements: Drafting, negotiating, executing, and enforcing lease terms and renewals.
      1. Facility Maintenance: Coordinating preventive maintenance, routine repairs, vendor contracts, and capital improvements.
      1. Facility Finances: Managing operating budgets, tracking income/expenses, handling security deposits, and reporting financial statements to the owner.
      1. Risk Management and Safety: Ensuring code compliance, maintaining insurance coverage, mitigating physical hazards, and protecting tenant safety.
  • Fiduciary Duties & Maximizing Investment Return
    • Fiduciary Obligations: Property managers act as general agents for the property owner and owe the standard OLD CAR fiduciary duties: Obedience, Loyalty, Disclosure, Confidentiality, Accounting, and Reasonable care.
    • Primary Financial Goal: To maximize the property owner's return on investment (ROI) by minimizing unnecessary expenditures, striving for 100% occupancy, and executing strategic capital improvements that increase property value and rental income.
  • Tennessee Licensing Requirements & Exemptions
    • General Rule: In Tennessee, individuals acting as third-party property managers must hold an active Tennessee real estate broker's license (many also pursue specialized credentials such as the Certified Property Manager / CPM designation).
    • Individuals Exempt from Holding a Broker's License:
      • Homeowners Association (HOA) property managers
      • Commercial property managers
      • Residential leasing agents or on-site resident managers
      • Individuals managing short-term rentals within a common interest community (e.g., timeshares)
    • Permitted Activities for Non-Licensees (Support Staff / Assistants / Maintenance):
      • Collecting lease applications, security deposits, or rent payments.
      • Showing rental units or executing pre-approved leases while acting under the direct supervision of the owner or brokerage firm.
      • Carrying out routine administrative, clerical, accounting/financial, or physical maintenance tasks.
  • Management Proposals & Owner Reporting
    • Management Proposal: A formal business plan prepared by a prospective property manager for a property owner to secure a management contract. It outlines a thorough market analysis, the property's current financial standing, a proposed operating budget, and concrete operational strategies.
    • Owner Reporting: Regular, detailed communication from the property manager to the owner tracking key property metrics—including maintenance updates, rent collections, financial statements, facility budgets, and notable incidents—to support transparent decision-making and owner collaboration.
  • The Management Agreement & Agency Relationships
    • Management Agreement (Property Management Contract): A binding written contract signed by both the property owner and the property manager that explicitly defines the manager's duties, powers, compensation, and operational/leasing responsibilities.
    • Establishing Agency: Signing a management agreement creates a formal principal-agent relationship (Owner = Principal; Property Manager = Agent), legally empowering the manager to act on the owner's behalf within defined boundaries.
  • Three Classifications of Agency in Property Management
    Agency ClassificationScope of AuthorityProperty Management / Real Estate Example
    Universal AgentUnlimited authority to perform any legal act the principal could do personallyAppointed via a broad Power of Attorney (rare in standard property management)
    General Agent (Most Common)Broad authority to manage all ongoing affairs within a specified business or propertyA full-service property manager empowered to sign leases, collect rent, hire vendors, and oversee daily operations
    Special AgentLimited authority restricted to a single specific transaction or narrow dutyA real estate salesperson listing a property, or a manager authorized only to collect rent
  • A Tale of Two Relationships & Fiduciary Priorities
    • Dual Role: A property manager bridges two distinct relationships:
      • With the Property Owner: Mirrors an affiliate broker working under a principal broker; governed by a fiduciary relationship requiring the highest level of trust and confidence.
      • With Tenants: Mirrors a landlord-tenant relationship; requires fair dealing, code compliance, and honoring lease obligations.
    • Primary Fiduciary Duty: While owing honesty and reasonable care to tenants and clients, the property manager's first and foremost fiduciary duty is to the property owner—encompassing reasonable skill, care, diligence, loyalty, obedience, confidentiality, accounting, and full disclosure.
  • Core Elements of Property Management Agreements
    • Property management contracts can be sourced from brokerages, professional trade associations, or drafted by real estate attorneys, and typically contain these essential provisions:
      ProvisionPurpose & Required DetailsKey Practical Considerations
      1. Identification of the PartiesLegal names of the property owner (matching the deed/title) and the property manager or management firmMust list all individual partners (if a partnership) or the exact corporate entity name (if owned by a corporation)
      2. Property DescriptionStreet address, full legal description, and square footageMust explicitly exclude any portions of the building not covered under management (e.g., an independent ground-floor retail store)
      3. Contract PeriodExact commencement date, total duration of the agreement, and renewal termsPrecision prevents liability disputes regarding when duties legally begin and end
      4. Termination ClausesConditions for ending the contract, required advance notice (e.g., 30, 60, or 90 days), and early termination penalties/feesClarifies acceptable grounds for early cancellation by either the owner or the manager
      5. Scope of AuthorityExplicit list of powers granted to the manager (collecting rent, setting rates, ordering repairs, hiring/firing staff, evicting tenants, pursuing legal action)Crucial Provision: Must explicitly authorize the property manager to sign leases on behalf of the owner

Key Terms

  • management agreement - a written contract between a property owner and a property manager to establish all duties of the property manager, including operation and leasing activities
  • management proposal - a plan that a property manager creates for managing the property that includes an analysis of the market along with the financial standing, operating budget, and other important factors of the property
  • property management - the vocation of renting or leasing someone else's real property on their behalf in exchange for compensation and in accordance with a property management employee contract

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Property Managers
    • A property manager is someone who is hired by a property owner to market, maintain, and oversee the day-to-day operation of a property (usually a rental property).
    • Their function is to oversee property operations on behalf of the owner and to realize the highest return possible on the owner's investment. There are three main types of property managers:
      • Individual property managers
      • Individual building managers
      • Resident managers
  • Main Duties of Property Managers
    • While there are many types of property to manage, all property managers can expect to share the same main duties, which include:
      • Marketing
      • Tenant relationships
      • Rent setting and collection
      • Lease agreements
      • Facility maintenance
      • Facility finances
      • Risk management and safety
  • Property Management Agreement
    • The management agreement is a written contract between a property owner and a property manager to establish all duties of the property manager, including operation and leasing activities.
    • There are some basic elements of property management agreements that are fairly universal and common amongst all uses. These include:
      • Identification of parties
      • Property description
      • Scope of authority
