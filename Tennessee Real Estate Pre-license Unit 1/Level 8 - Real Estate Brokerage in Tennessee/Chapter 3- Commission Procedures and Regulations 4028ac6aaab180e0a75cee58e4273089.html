Chapter 3- Commission Procedures and Regulations

  • Definition of Terms
    • Compensation: Any fee, commission, salary, money, or other valuable consideration—or the promise of consideration—given for real estate services rendered.
    • Commission: A fee paid for real estate brokerage services, typically calculated as a percentage of the final sales price, which serves as the primary income source for real estate agents.
  • Commission Structure & Negotiation
    • Firm Independence: Every brokerage sets its own internal commission policies and splits independently.
    • Mutual Agreement: Commission rates are always fully negotiable between the broker and the client for each individual transaction; clients are free to seek services elsewhere if they disagree with a brokerage's policies.
  • Prohibition Against Price-Fixing (Antitrust Law)
    • The Sherman Antitrust Act of 1890: Explicitly prohibits collusion, price-fixing, or agreements between competing brokerages to establish uniform commission rates within a market area (e.g., Bumblee Brokerage and Ladybug Realty operating completely independently without discussing fee structures).
  • Types of Broker Compensation
    • Percentage of Sales Price (Most Common): The broker receives an agreed-upon percentage of the final sales price, paid out of the seller's proceeds at closing.
    • Flat Fee: The broker receives a predetermined, fixed dollar amount for a property transaction (e.g., a flat $1,000 fee).
    • Fee-for-Service (Menu-of-Services): The consumer selects and pays for specific, individual menu items or a la carte services rather than a full-service package.
    • Timing of Payment: Brokers are generally compensated at closing from transaction proceeds according to the terms outlined in the listing or buyer representation agreement.
  • How Affiliate Brokers Receive Payment
    • The Principal Broker Route: Because all listings and transactions technically belong to the principal broker, any compensation paid upon a sale goes directly to the principal broker first.
    • The Commission Split: The principal broker then pays the affiliate broker their agreed-upon portion (the "split") according to the terms of their independent contractor or employment agreement.
    • Strict Payment Rule: An affiliate broker can never accept compensation directly from a client, customer, or outside party. All checks or funds must be paid to the brokerage, and the affiliate broker can only be paid by their own principal broker (e.g., if a client attempts to hand an agent a check directly, the agent must politely direct them to pay the broker).
  • Out-of-State Broker Referrals
    • A licensed Tennessee broker is permitted to pay a commission or referral fee to a licensed broker in another state, provided that the out-of-state broker does not personally conduct any active negotiations for the Tennessee transaction.
  • Compensation Does Not Determine Agency
    • Receiving payment from a party does not automatically establish an agency relationship with that party. For example, a buyer's agent (Larry) can legally receive their commission payout funded by the seller (Sera) at closing without altering who they legally represent.
  • Post-Termination Commission Rights
    • Commissions earned by an affiliate broker while they were actively working under a principal broker remain legally payable to that affiliate broker even if they subsequently:
    • Transfer to a new brokerage.
    • Retire their real estate license.
    • Enter broker release status.
    • Allow their license to expire.
    • Pass away (payable to their estate).
  • Anatomy of a Mathematical Formula
    • Definition: A formula is a specific mathematical expression used to solve for a particular value by expressing the relationship between different components.
    • Example (Rectangle Area):
      • Length x Width = Area
  • Formula Configurations & Rearrangement
    • Because mathematical relationships remain constant, formulas can be reconfigured based on known variables to solve for whatever unknown value is required, ensuring you only need to learn each core formula once.
    • Alternative Configurations (Rectangle Area Example):
      • Area ÷ Length = Width
      • Area ÷ Width = Length
    • The Swiss Army Knife Analogy: Think of a formula as a single tool with multiple parts; you simply select the configuration or "blade" that fits the specific job at hand.
  • Definition of Percent
    • "Percent" originates from the Latin phrase per centum, meaning "per hundred" (e.g., 80% means 80 out of 100).
  • The Golden Rule of Real Estate Math
    • You can never solve a math problem while a number is in percentage form; it must always be converted into its decimal form first.
  • Steps to Convert a Percentage to a Decimal
    1. Eliminate the percent sign: Drop the % symbol (e.g., 35% becomes 35).
    1. Move the decimal two spaces to the left: Since the number is divided by 100 (which has two powers of 10), the decimal point—which always starts immediately to the right of the whole number—moves two places to the left (e.g., 35 becomes 0.35).
  • Converting Single-Digit Percentages to Decimals
    • Just like double-digit percentages, single-digit percentages must be converted to decimals before solving any math problems.
    • Steps for Conversion:
      1. Eliminate the percent sign: Drop the % symbol (e.g., 7% becomes 7).
      1. Move the decimal two spaces to the left and add a placeholder zero: Start with the decimal point to the right of the whole number, move it two places to the left, and fill any empty space with a zero (e.g., 7 becomes 0.07).
  • Commission Formula & Configurations
    • Commission problems utilize a core three-part relationship (where Total = Sales Price, Percentage = Commission Rate, and Part = Commission Amount/Total Payout).
    • Total x Percentage = Part
    • Part ÷ Percentage = Total
    • Part ÷ Total = Percentage
    • Step-by-Step Problem Solving Method (Solving for Part)
      1. Identify Known Variables: Determine the total sales price and the commission percentage rate.
      1. Convert Percentage to Decimal: Eliminate the percent sign and move the decimal two places to the left (e.g., 3% becomes 0.03).
      1. Multiply Total by Decimal: Multiply the total sales price by the decimal rate to find the monetary part/commission amount (e.g., $250,000 x 0.03 = $7,500).
  • Solving for Commission Rate (Percentage)
    • Part ÷ Total = Percentage
  • Steps to Solve:
  1. Insert Known Variables: Place the commission amount (Part) and sales price (Total) into the formula (e.g., $7,500 ÷ $250,000 = Percentage).
  1. Divide Part by Total: Perform the division to get the decimal form (e.g., $7,500 ÷ $250,000 = 0.03).
  1. Convert Decimal to Percentage: Move the decimal point two spaces to the right, drop the decimal, and add a percent sign (e.g., 0.03 becomes 3%).
  • Commission Splits (Affiliate Payout Calculations)
  • Definition: The commission split is the agreed-upon percentage payout distributed from the principal broker to the affiliate sales agent based on their independent contractor or employment agreement.
  • Step-by-Step Multi-Step Problem Solving Method:
  1. Calculate Total Brokerage Commission: Multiply the full sales price by the specific broker's commission percentage rate to find the firm's total commission earnings (e.g., Total x Percentage = Part → $85,000 x 0.03 = $2,550).
  1. Apply the Agent Split Rate: Treat the firm's commission total as the new "Total," and multiply it by the agent's commission split percentage (converted to a decimal) to find the agent's payout (e.g., $2,550 x 0.45 = $1,147.50).
  • Solving for Commission Rate (Percentage)
    • Part ÷ Total = Percentage
    • Steps to Solve:
      1. Insert Known Variables: Place the commission amount (Part) and sales price (Total) into the formula (e.g., $7,500 ÷ $250,000 = Percentage).
      1. Divide Part by Total: Perform the division to get the decimal form (e.g., $7,500 ÷ $250,000 = 0.03).
      1. Convert Decimal to Percentage: Move the decimal point two spaces to the right, drop the decimal, and add a percent sign (e.g., 0.03 becomes 3%).

  • Key Term
    • Commission - A fee paid in exchange for professional services rendered in a real estate transaction.

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Commission Structure & Price-Fixing Prohibition
    • Each brokerage establishes its own independent commission structure. Collusion or agreements between competing firms to set uniform commission rates within a market area constitutes illegal price-fixing under the Sherman Antitrust Act.
  • Strict Payment Rules for Affiliate Brokers
    • An affiliate broker can only receive compensation from their principal broker. They are strictly prohibited from accepting direct payments, bonuses, or checks from clients or outside parties.
  • Mandatory Compensation & Referral Fee Disclosures
    • All forms of compensation, special bonuses, and referral fees (both given and received) must be fully disclosed to all relevant parties and clients.
  • TREC Limitations on Commission Disputes
    • The Tennessee Real Estate Commission does not arbitrate or intervene in financial debts, commission disputes, draws, or loans between firms, brokers, or affiliates; these matters must be resolved through a local association of REALTORS or via civil litigation.
  • Commission Math Formulas
    • Part ÷ Percentage = Total
    • Total x Percentage = Part
    • Part ÷ Total = Percentage
