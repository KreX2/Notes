Chapter 3 - Measuring Real Property

Common Measurement Conversions

  • 1 yard = 3 feet
  • 1 mile = 5,280 feet
  • 1 sq. yard = 9 sq. feet
  • 1 acre = 43,560 sq. feet
  • 1 sq. mile = 640 acres
  • Square Footage
    • Definition: A unit of area measurement equal to a square measuring 1 foot by 1 foot (1 sq. ft.).
    • Importance: The most common measurement buyers consider when searching for and evaluating a home.
    • Usage: Used by buyers and sellers as a standard metric to compare the relative sizes of different buildings.
  • Square Footage Comparison (Practical Application)
    • Comparing Property Value: Homes listed at the same price with identical bedroom and bathroom counts (e.g., $275,000 for 3 bd / 2 ba) can offer significantly different amounts of square footage, affecting value per square foot.
    • Factors Beyond Square Footage: While square footage provides a useful size benchmark, it is not the only factor determining a home's overall value and appeal. Other critical considerations include:
      • Property Condition: A smaller property may be fully updated or better maintained than a larger home in poorer condition.
      • Layout and Functionality: An efficient, well-designed layout can make a smaller home feel larger and function better than a larger home with poor flow or wasted space.
    • Math Practice: Calculating Square Footage
      • Formula: Length times Width = Area (Square Footage)
      • Example Problem: Calculate the square footage of a room measuring 15 ft. long by 11 ft. wide.
      • Step-by-Step Solution:
        • Step 1: Identify known variables.
          • Length = 15 ft.
          • Width = 11 ft.
        • Step 2: Multiply length by width.
          • 15 ft. x 11 ft. = 165 sq. ft.
      • Final Answer: The room is 165 sq. ft.
  • Livable Area
    • Finished Area (Livable Area): Space that is enclosed, heated and cooled, and safe and comfortable for year-round human habitation.
      • Examples: Finished basements, climate-controlled sunrooms, or converted attic bedrooms.
    • Unfinished Area (Non-Livable): External or non-climate-controlled spaces that cannot be counted toward a home's total livable area.
      • Examples: Garages, patios, decks, porches, screened-in porches, and unfinished basements or storage attics.
    • Math Practice: Calculating Livable Area
      • Formula: Total Area - Unfinished Area= Livable Area
      • Example Problem: A home has total dimensions equal to 1,000 sq. ft., which includes an attached garage measuring 10 ft. by 10 ft. Calculate the livable area.
      • Step-by-Step Solution:
        • Step 1: Find the unfinished garage area 10 ft. times 10ft. = 100sq. ft.
        • Step 2: Subtract the unfinished area from the total house area 1,000sq. ft. - 100sq. ft. = 900 sq. ft.
      • Final Answer: The true livable area is 900 sq. ft.
  • Usable Square Footage (USF)
    • The total measurement of space that a tenant exclusively occupies (also known as net square footage). It is space that is not shared with any other tenants.
    • Examples:
      • An office suite where a firm's employees work
      • A specific warehouse section used for manufacturing or storage
      • A retail shop located in a shopping mall
    • BOMA Method for Measuring USF:
      • The standard measurement method developed by the Building Owners and Managers Association, used across most of the U.S.
      • Calculated by measuring from the finished surface of the office side of permanent walls to the center of any partitions that separate the tenant's office from adjoining tenant or usable areas.
  • Rentable Square Footage (RSF)
    • The total measurement of space a tenant can access (also known as gross square footage). It includes the tenant's usable square footage (USF) plus their proportion of shared building common areas.
    • Common Areas Included:
      • Ground floor lobbies
      • Shared restrooms and hallways
      • Elevators and elevator shafts
      • Shared parking lots
    • Key Formula: Usable Square Footage (USF) + Common Areas = Rentable Square Footage (RSF)
  • Don't Personally Calculate Square Footage for Listings
    • Why Agents Shouldn't Hand-Calculate:
      • Different jurisdictions and local markets have varying rules for measuring square footage (e.g., measuring from exterior walls vs. interior walls) and defining what counts as livable space.
      • Official square footage calculations must always be performed by a qualified professional, such as a licensed surveyor or a floor plan specialist.
    • Citing Official Sources:
      • When providing square footage in a property listing or official documentation, agents must use and cite an official source (e.g., "according to [Source], the square footage is [Y]").
    • Including hand-calculated or unverified square footage numbers can lead to lawsuits and legal liability for misrepresentation.
  • Triangular Lots
    • Value Consideration: Triangular-shaped lots are usually more valuable if the longer leg (base) abuts the street it is located on.
    • Formula: 1/2 x Base x Height = Area (or [Base x Height] / 2 = Area)
    • Math Practice: Calculating the Area of a Triangle
      • Example Problem: Calculate the area of a triangular lot with a base of 54 ft. and a height of 80 ft.
      • Step-by-Step Solution:
        • Step 1: Plug the base and height into the formula.
        • Step 2: Multiply 1/2 x 54 ft. x 80 ft. = 2,160 sq. ft.
      • Final Answer: The area of the lot is 2,160 sq. ft.
  • Irregular Lots
    • Homes and lots are rarely a single simple shape; they are usually a combination of different geometric shapes (such as rectangles, squares, and triangles).
    • Method for Calculating Area: Divide the irregularly shaped space or floor plan into smaller, standard geometric shapes.
    • Total Area Formula: Total Area = Sum of the areas of all individual smaller shapes/rooms.
    • Math Practice: Calculating Area of Irregular Lots
      • Strategy: Break the irregular shape into smaller, standard shapes (e.g., two rectangles), determine any missing side dimensions, calculate each area individually, and add the areas together.
      • Step-by-Step Solution:
        • Step 1: Find missing dimensions and calculate the area of the first shape (top rectangle).
          • Find missing side length: 50 ft. - 30 ft. = 20 ft.
          • Calculate top rectangle area: 20 ft. x 25 ft. = 500 sq. ft.
        • Step 2: Calculate the area of the second shape (bottom rectangle).
          • Calculate bottom rectangle area: 40 ft. x 30 ft. = 1,200 sq. ft.
        • Step 3: Add the individual areas together to find the total lot size.
          • Total area: 500 sq. ft. + 1,200 sq. ft. = 1,700 sq. ft.
      • Final Answer: The total area of the irregular lot is 1,700 sq. ft.
      • To find the total area of this irregular lot, calculate the area of the rectangle and the right triangle separately, then add them together:
        1. Area of the Rectangle (Yellow):
          Area = 15 ft. x 36 ft. = 540 sq. ft.
        1. Area of the Right Triangle (Pink):
          Area = 1/2 x 23 ft. x 24 ft. = 276 sq. ft.
        1. Total Area:
          Total Area = 540 sq. ft. + 276 sq. ft. = 816 sq. ft.
  • Don't Calculate a House's Square Footage
    • Layout Complexity: Calculating a house's square footage is much more complicated than basic area formulas because homes feature multiple corners, halls, and irregular layouts.
    • Risk of Liability: License holders who incorrectly calculate a home's square footage can be held legally liable for misrepresentation.
    • Best Practice: License holders should always search for, verify, and cite an official source for square footage rather than attempting to calculate it themselves.
  • Price Per Square Foot (PPSF)
    • Definition & Utility:
      • Allows home shoppers to make direct "apples-to-apples" price comparisons between properties of different sizes.
      • Helps determine if a property is priced appropriately for its specific neighborhood.
      • Seasoned agents often know the ballpark price per square foot for specific local neighborhoods.
    • Formula: Asking Price / Total Square Feet = Price per Square Foot
      • Note: This same formula structure applies to finding price per square yard, price per acre, or price per unit (Asking Price / Total Units = Price per Unit).
    • Math Practice / Example:
      • Scenario: Enrique paid $250,000 for a 1,500 sq. ft. home. Calculate the price per square foot.
      • Calculation: $250,000 / 1,500 sq. ft. = $166.67 per sq. ft.
      • Final Answer: Enrique paid $166.67 per sq. ft.
    • Kevin's House
      • Kevin wants a house near his office priced at less than $200 per square foot. He views a property listed at $252,000 with 1,105 sq. ft.
      • Calculation:
        • Formula: Asking Price / Total Square Feet = Price per Square Foot
        • Equation: $252,000 / 1,105 sq. ft. = $228.05 per sq. ft.
      • Outcome: At $228.05 per sq. ft., this house does NOT meet Kevin's requirement of being under $200 per sq. ft.
      • Factors Influencing Price Per Square Foot: PPSF varies based on multiple property characteristics. For instance, a brand-new home generally commands a higher price per square foot because it is in superior condition and less likely to need immediate repairs.
    • Math Practice: Solving for Total Asking Price / Rent
      • Formula: Total Square Feet x Price per Square Foot = Total Asking Price (or Total Rent)
      • Math Practice / Example:
        • Scenario: A tenant wants to lease 1,500 sq. ft. of warehouse space at $3 per square foot per year. Calculate the total annual rent.
        • Step-by-Step Solution:
          • Step 1: Identify known variables (1,500 sq. ft. and $3 per sq. ft.).
          • Step 2: Multiply the total square footage by the price per square foot:
            1,500 sq. ft. x $3 = $4,500
        • Final Answer: The tenant would spend $4,500 per year to rent all of the warehouse space.
  • Measuring the Volume of Structures
    • Concept: While square footage measures 2D surface area (length x width), volume measures the total 3D space enclosed within a structure or container. Volume is measured in cubic feet.
    • Formula: Length x Width x Height = Volume (in cubic feet)
    • Math Practice / Example:
      • Scenario: Calculate the volume of a structure measuring 50 ft. long, 20 ft. wide, and 30 ft. high.
      • Calculation: 50 ft. x 20 ft. x 30 ft. = 30,000 cubic ft.
    • Square Feet vs. Cubic Feet Comparison:
      • Surface Area (Square Footage): 50 ft. x 20 ft. = 1,000 sq. ft.
      • Total Volume (Cubic Feet): 50 ft. x 20 ft. x 30 ft. = 30,000 cubic ft.
    • Real-World Application: While residential buyers primarily focus on square footage, measuring volume is vital in commercial real estate to calculate the storage capacity of warehouses, industrial spaces, or storage units.
  • Frontage (Front Feet) and Depth
    • The portion of a property's boundary that directly borders a street or road.
    • Front Foot: A unit of measurement for street frontage equal to one linear foot along the street.
    • Dimension Convention: Frontage is ALWAYS stated first in property dimensions.
      • Example: In a property dimension written as 150 ft. x 313 ft., the frontage is 150 front feet (150 ft.), and the depth is 313 ft.
    • Depth: The measurement of how far back a lot extends from the front street boundary to the rear boundary.
    • Math Practice: Calculating Front Feet
      • Concept: When you know the total square footage (area) and the depth of a rectangular lot, you can solve for the frontage by dividing the total area by the depth.
      • Formula: Area / Depth = Frontage (Front Feet)
      • Math Practice / Example:
        • Scenario: A lot has a total area of 46,905 sq. ft. and a depth of 313 ft. Calculate its frontage.
        • Calculation: 46,905 sq. ft. / 313 ft. = 150 front feet
      • Final Answer: The lot has a frontage of 150 front feet.
    • Math Practice: Frontage of Multiple Lots
      • Formula: Area / Depth = Frontage (Front Feet)
      • Example Problem: Three adjacent lots on Sleepy Street each have a depth of 130 ft. Calculate the frontage for each lot and the total combined frontage for the entire street.
        • Lot 1 Area: 19,500 sq. ft.
        • Lot 2 Area: 18,070 sq. ft.
        • Lot 3 Area: 11,960 sq. ft.
      • Step-by-Step Solution:
        • Step 1: Divide each lot's square footage by the shared depth (130 ft.) to find individual frontages.
          • Lot 1 Frontage: 19,500 sq. ft. / 130 ft. = 150 front feet
          • Lot 2 Frontage: 18,070 sq. ft. / 130 ft. = 139 front feet
          • Lot 3 Frontage: 11,960 sq. ft. / 130 ft. = 92 front feet
        • Step 2: Add the individual frontages together to find the total street frontage.
          • Total Frontage: 150 front feet + 139 front feet + 92 front feet = 381 front feet
      • Final Answer:
        • Individual Frontages: Lot 1 = 150 front feet, Lot 2 = 139 front feet, Lot 3 = 92 front feet
        • Total Street Frontage: 381 front feet
    • Price Per Square Foot Scenario: Indecisive Iggy
      • Scenario Overview: Comparing three different properties for a client using Price Per Square Foot (PPSF) to make an "apples-to-apples" comparison.
      • Formula: Asking Price / Total Square Feet = Price per Square Foot
      • Property Comparison & Calculations:
        • The Roommate House:
          • Features: 4 beds, 2.5 baths, 2,400 sq. ft.
          • Asking Price: $360,000
          • Calculation: $360,000 / 2,400 sq. ft. = $150.00 per sq. ft.
        • The Studio:
          • Features: 1 bed, 1 bath, 700 sq. ft.
          • Asking Price: $110,000
          • Calculation: $110,000 / 700 sq. ft. = $157.14 per sq. ft.
        • The Duplex:
          • Features: 2 beds, 2 baths, 1,300 sq. ft.
          • Asking Price: $224,000
          • Calculation: $224,000 / 1,300 sq. ft. = $172.31 per sq. ft.
      • Value Takeaway:
        • Lowest PPSF (Best value per square foot): The Roommate House at $150.00/sq. ft.
        • Mid-range PPSF: The Studio at $157.14/sq. ft.
        • Highest PPSF: The Duplex at $172.31/sq. ft.
    • Applied Math Practice: Indecisive Iggy's Property Requirements
      • Calculating Lot Frontage (Front Feet)
        • Scenario: Iggy needs to confirm if his 8,520 sq. ft. lot with a depth of 95 ft. has enough street frontage for a 20 ft. holiday display.
        • Formula: Total Square Footage / Depth = Frontage (Front Feet)
        • Calculation: 8,520 sq. ft. / 95 ft. = 89.68 front feet
        • Outcome: The lot provides approximately 89.68 ft. of frontage, which is plenty of space.
      • Calculating Individual Room Square Footage (From Official Floor Plans)
        • Agent Rule: When evaluating individual room dimensions with clients, agents should rely on official floor plans created by professionals rather than taking or hand-calculating their own measurements.
        • Requirement: Iggy wants bedrooms to be at least 170 sq. ft. to fit a king-sized bed.
        • Room Calculations (Length x Width = Area):
          • Room 1: 14 ft. x 12.5 ft. = 175 sq. ft. (Exceeds 170 sq. ft.)
          • Room 2: 13 ft. x 13 ft. = 169 sq. ft. (1 sq. ft. under requirement, accepted by client)
          • Room 3: 15 ft. x 12 ft. = 180 sq. ft. (Exceeds 170 sq. ft.)
          • Room 4: 14 ft. x 13.5 ft. = 189 sq. ft. (Exceeds 170 sq. ft.)
        • Outcome: Three of the four bedrooms exceed 170 sq. ft., and the fourth is 169 sq. ft.
      • Comparing Storage Volume (Storage Unit vs. Storage Shed)
        • Scenario: Compare a 10 ft. x 10 ft. x 8 ft. storage unit against a 12 ft. x 8 ft. x 9 ft. outdoor shed to determine which has more capacity.
        • Formula: Length x Width x Height = Volume (Cubic Feet)
        • Step 1 (Storage Unit Volume): 10 ft. x 10 ft. x 8 ft. = 800 cubic ft.
        • Step 2 (Storage Shed Volume): 12 ft. x 8 ft. x 9 ft. = 864 cubic ft.
        • Step 3 (Compare): 864 cubic ft. > 800 cubic ft.
        • Outcome: The backyard storage shed has 64 cubic ft. more capacity than the storage unit.
  • Acreage (Land Size)
    • Definition: A unit of area measurement used to quantify the size of land.
    • Key Conversion Factor: 1 acre = 43,560 sq. ft.
    • Math Workshop : Jenny's Farm
      • Square Footage Calculation:
        • Formula: Length x Width = Total Square Feet
        • Calculation: 1,386 ft. x 451 ft. = 625,086 sq. ft.
      • Acreage Conversion:
        • Formula: Total Square Feet / 43,560 sq. ft. = Acreage
        • Calculation: 625,086 sq. ft. / 43,560 sq. ft. = 14.35 acres
      • Final Summary: Jenny's farm measures 625,086 sq. ft., which converts to 14.35 acres.
    • Math Practice: Price per Square Yard (Blake's Lot)
      • Given Information:
        • Lot Size: 2.5 acres
        • Total Purchase Price: $500,000
      • Step-by-Step Solution:
        • Step 1: Convert acres to square feet.
          • Formula: Acres x 43,560 = Total Square Feet
          • Calculation: 2.5 acres x 43,560 sq. ft. = 108,900 sq. ft.
        • Step 2: Convert square feet to square yards.
          • Formula: Total Square Feet / 9 = Total Square Yards
          • Calculation: 108,900 sq. ft. / 9 = 12,100 sq. yds.
        • Step 3: Calculate price per square yard.
          • Formula: Total Price / Total Square Yards = Price per Square Yard
          • Calculation: $500,000 / 12,100 sq. yds. = $41.32 per sq. yd.
      • Final Answer: Blake paid $41.32 per square yard.
    • Math Practice: Price per Acre (Sophie's Lot)
      • Given Information:
        • Lot Dimensions: 300 ft. wide x 500 ft. long
        • Total Purchase Price: $275,000
      • Step-by-Step Solution:
        • Step 1: Calculate the total square footage of the lot.
          • Formula: Length x Width = Total Square Feet
          • Calculation: 300 ft. x 500 ft. = 150,000 sq. ft.
        • Step 2: Convert square footage to acreage.
          • Formula: Total Square Feet / 43,560 = Total Acres
          • Calculation: 150,000 sq. ft. / 43,560 sq. ft. = 3.44 acres
        • Step 3: Calculate the price per acre.
          • Formula: Total Price / Total Acres = Price per Acre
          • Calculation: $275,000 / 3.44 acres = $79,941.86 per acre
      • Final Answer: Sophie paid $79,941.86 per acre.
  • Math Practice: Finding Perimeter
    • Concept & Definition:
      • Perimeter is the total distance around the outside boundary of a shape, calculated by adding together the lengths of all its sides.
      • In real estate, perimeter defines the complete outer boundary of a property.
    • Formulas:
      • General Formula: Side 1 + Side 2 + Side 3 + Side 4 = Perimeter
      • Square Formula: 4 x Side Length = Perimeter
      • Rectangle Formula: (2 x Length) + (2 x Width) = Perimeter
    • Math Practice / Examples:
      • Example 1: A Square Lot
        • Scenario: Calculate the perimeter of a square lot measuring 100 ft. on each of its four sides.
        • Calculation: 100 ft. + 100 ft. + 100 ft. + 100 ft. = 400 ft.
        • Final Answer: The perimeter of the square lot is 400 ft.
      • Example 2: A Rectangular Lot
        • Scenario: Calculate the perimeter of a rectangular lot with a length of 200 ft. and a width of 50 ft.
        • Calculation: 200 ft. + 200 ft. + 50 ft. + 50 ft. = 500 ft.
        • Final Answer: The perimeter of the rectangular lot is 500 ft.
      • Example 3: Perry's Lot
        • Concept: To find the perimeter of an irregular lot, sum the lengths of all individual boundary sides around the outer perimeter.
        • Given Dimensions: 50 ft., 30 ft., 30 ft., 20 ft., 20 ft., and 50 ft.
        • Step-by-Step Solution:
          • Step 1: Identify all side lengths surrounding the property.
          • Step 2: Add all side lengths together:
            50 ft. + 30 ft. + 30 ft. + 20 ft. + 20 ft. + 50 ft. = 200 ft.
        • Final Answer: The total perimeter of Perry's irregular lot is 200 ft.
