Chapter 3 - Consumer Protection

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) Act & Truth in Advertising

  • Statutory Purpose: Enacted in 1914, the Federal Trade Commission Act established the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) as the nation's consumer protection agency, enforcing federal truth-in-advertising and consumer protection laws nationwide.
  • Three Core Truth-in-Advertising Provisions:
    FTC ProvisionLegal RequirementReal Estate Examples & Applications
    1. Truthful and Non-DeceptiveAdvertisements must tell the truth and must not mislead consumers either through false statements or by omitting material information.• Do not advertise a home with a hole in the roof as having "a lot of natural light."

    • Do not advertise a broker-listed property as "For Sale By Owner" (FSBO).
    2. Backed Up With EvidenceAdvertisers must have objective proof/substantiation to support all factual claims made in an ad.• If an ad claims a home was "built in the Victorian era" or cites specific square footage, be prepared to verify and prove it with documentation.
    3. FairAdvertisements must fairly represent the property or service being offered without causing unavoidable consumer injury.• Do not hide or omit mandatory fees (such as application fees or utility charges) that leave consumers blindsided and financially harmed.
  • Practical Real Estate Advertising Compliance Rules:
    • Use actual unit photos: For rental properties, display photographs of the actual available units in their true condition rather than a more upscale model apartment.
    • State accurate price ranges: Ensure advertised prices or rent ranges reflect what units actually sell or lease for.
    • Disclose all fees and policies upfront: Clearly state application fees, utility fees, and property policies (e.g., no-smoking or pet policies).
    • Avoid misleading imagery: Do not include photos or graphics implying amenities that do not exist (e.g., showing someone holding a tennis racket when the property has no tennis court).
  • Prohibition on Bait-and-Switch Advertising:
    • Definition: Advertising a property, price, or service with no genuine intention of selling or leasing it on the advertised terms, solely to lure prospects in and switch them to a higher-priced offer.
    • Example: Advertising an apartment at $200/month ($1,000 below market rate) to generate inquiries and then telling callers the actual rent is $1,200/month is an illegal bait-and-switch tactic.
  • Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) & Do Not Call Registry
    • Scope of the TCPA: Regulates commercial telephone solicitations—including calls soliciting participation in a real estate transaction—and governs the use of:
      • Autodialers
      • Fax machines
      • Voice messaging / prerecorded systems
    • Unsolicited Advertisements:
      • Any material advertisement offering a good or service (such as a property listing or property management services) sent or placed to another person without their prior consent, permission, or invitation.
    • National Do Not Call (DNC) Registry Compliance Rules:
      • 31-Day Removal / Scrubbing Rule: When a consumer registers their phone number on the National Do Not Call Registry, a brokerage must remove that number from its calling list within 31 days.
      • Mandatory List Scrubbing: Brokers and agents must check the Do Not Call Registry before making solicitation calls and scrub/update their call lists at least once every quarter (and within 31 days of dialing).
      • Permissible Calling Hours: Telemarketing and solicitation calls may only be placed between 8:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m. in the recipient's local time.
  • TCPA Telemarketing Identification & Caller ID Rules
    • Under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), any telemarketing caller must immediately and clearly disclose three pieces of information:
      1. Who they are: The caller's own name (e.g., "Hi, I'm Crystal Shan DeLier.").
      1. For whom they are calling: The name of the brokerage or entity on whose behalf the call is made (e.g., "I am an agent for Lit Realty.").
      1. How they can be contacted: A valid telephone number or address at which the caller or brokerage can be reached (e.g., "You can reach me at (608) 123-1234.").
    • No Blocked Caller ID: Telemarketers are strictly prohibited from blocking their phone numbers on Caller ID.
    • Immediate Opt-Out Compliance: Callers must promptly place any person who asks not to be called onto the brokerage's internal Do Not Call list—even if that person is not registered on the national Do Not Call Registry.
  • Established Business Relationship & Inquiry Exemptions
    • Even if a consumer's phone number is listed on the National Do Not Call Registry, limited calling windows apply when the consumer initiates contact with a brokerage:
      Contact TypePermissible Calling WindowExamples & Conditions
      Established Business RelationshipUp to 18 months following the establishment of the business relationship (or last transaction/activity)• A client who signs a listing agreement, buyer representation agreement, or property management contract with the brokerage.
      Business Inquiry / ApplicationUp to 3 months following the date of the consumer's inquiry• A visitor signing in at an open house.

      • A prospect contacting a broker or sales associate to request information about marketing or buying a property.
  • Absolute Override Rule (Internal Do Not Call Request): Regardless of an active 18-month business relationship or 3-month inquiry window—and regardless of whether the person is on the national registry—if a consumer asks to be removed from your call list, you must honor their request immediately and stop calling.
  • Regulatory Enforcement, Fines & Scenario Analysis
    • Enforcing Agencies: The Do Not Call Registry and telemarketing rules are jointly regulated and enforced by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and the Federal Communications Commission (FCC).
    • Penalties for Violations:
      • Curriculum Exam Standard: Each illegal telemarketing call can result in civil penalties of up to $16,000 per violation (note: under current federal inflation adjustments, FTC civil penalties for Do Not Call Registry violations exceed $50,000 per call, while private TCPA lawsuits allow consumers to recover $500 to $1,500 per willful violation).
    • Scenario Breakdown (Wayne and Phone Home Realty):
      • Homeowner #1 (Interested): Expresses interest in selling; Wayne may legally continue following up and pursuing the listing.
      • Homeowner #2 (Do Not Call Request): Explicitly states they are not interested in selling, are registered on the National Do Not Call Registry, and requests to be added to Phone Home Realty's internal Do Not Call list. By ignoring the opt-out request and continuing to place unsolicited calls, Wayne exposes himself and his brokerage to severe regulatory fines (cited at up to $16,000 per illegal call in the course material).
  • Phone Calls: Let's Recap
  • CAN-SPAM Act (2003)
    • Full Name & Purpose: Passed in 2003 and enforced by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), the Controlling the Assault of Non-Solicited Pornography and Marketing (CAN-SPAM) Act sets nationwide standards for commercial email advertising, grants recipients the right to refuse future commercial emails, and imposes costly penalties on businesses that violate its rules.
    • Mandatory Requirements (What Commercial Emails MUST Include):
      • Clear opt-out / unsubscribe option: Must provide a visible, easy way to opt out of future marketing emails, and all opt-out requests must be honored within 10 business days.
      • Valid physical postal address: Must include the sender's current physical street address, registered P.O. Box, or registered commercial mailbox.
      • Advertisement disclosure: Must clearly and conspicuously identify the message as an advertisement or solicitation.
      • Relevant content: Must include at least one sentence of relevant content.
      • Accurate sender identification: Must use clear, accurate routing and header information—including "From," "To," "Reply-To," domain names, and email addresses—that truthfully identifies the initiating person or business.
    • Prohibited Practices (What Commercial Emails MUST NOT Do):
      • Send undesired commercial messages to a customer's email address or wireless device after they have opted out.
      • Use false or misleading header information.
      • Use deceptive or misleading subject lines: The subject line must accurately reflect the actual content of the email (e.g., an agent putting "Free Pizza!" in the subject line of a property listing email when no free pizza is being offered violates the CAN-SPAM Act).
  • Junk Fax Prevention Act
    • Regulatory Authority: Enforced by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) to govern commercial fax transmissions.
    • Rules for Unsolicited Fax Advertisements:
      • Prior Permission / Established Relationship Required: Unsolicited advertisements cannot be sent to a residential or business fax machine unless the sender has the recipient's prior permission (or meets statutory established-business-relationship rules).
      • Mandatory Opt-Out Notice: Commercial faxes must include a clear opt-out notice explaining how the recipient can stop future faxes.
    • General Identification Rules (Apply to ALL Faxes, Including Solicited Faxes):
      • Every fax transmission must display:
        1. The date and time the fax is sent
        1. The sender's legal name (individual or registered business name)
        1. The telephone number of the sending machine or the sender
  • Tennessee State Consumer Protection & Telemarketing Laws
    • Tennessee Consumer Protection Act of 1977 (TCPTA):
      • Prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of trade or commerce in Tennessee.
      • Criminal Classification: Violations of the Act constitute Class B misdemeanors under Tennessee law.
    • Tennessee Do Not Call Program:
      • Operates alongside federal telemarketing rules to restrict unsolicited marketing calls and messages to Tennessee residents:
        • General Prohibition & Fines: Prohibits most unsolicited marketing calls, carrying fines of up to $2,000 per incident.
        • Caller Identification & Calling Hours: Solicitors must state their name and their organization or business at the very beginning of the call, and calls may only be placed between 8:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m..
        • Five Statutory Exemptions:
          1. Calls where the recipient has given prior, explicit permission to be called
          1. Calls made by non-profit organizations
          1. Political telemarketing calls
          1. Calls to existing customers with whom the business had a prior business relationship within the last 12 months (note: stricter than the federal 18-month window)
          1. Calls not intended to solicit or sell that are made no more than three times per week
  • Definition of "Advertising" Under Tennessee License Law (TREC Rule 1260-02-.12)
    • What Counts as Advertising: Encompasses traditional print, radio, and television ads, as well as any public-facing communication medium—including signs, flyers, letterheads, email signatures, websites, social media communications, and video or audio recordings transmitted via internet or broadcast streaming.
    • What Is Excluded (Promotional Materials): Items that merely promote a licensee without advertising specific property or brokerage services are exempt from the rule, including hats, pens, notepads, t-shirts, name tags, business cards, and sponsorships of charitable and community events.
  • Tennessee Online & Website Advertising Guidelines
    • Conspicuous Firm Identification: Every web page must conspicuously display the brokerage's licensed name (in lettering at least as large as any individual licensee or team name) and the brokerage's telephone number as registered on file with the Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC).
    • Third-Party / IDX Database Disclaimer: Every web page displaying property listings pulled from an outside database (such as an IDX feed) must clearly state that some or all of the listings may not belong to the brokerage whose site is being visited.
    • First-Generation vs. Syndicated Ads:
      • First-generation advertising (ads directly created and placed by a licensee or brokerage) must be kept accurate and up to date.
      • Licensees are not held responsible for inaccuracies in syndicated or aggregated third-party ads outside of the licensee's control and ability to monitor.
  • Social Media & The "One Click Away" Rule
    • How It Applies to Social Media: All standard Tennessee advertising rules apply to social media posts and ads, except that the required brokerage identification (firm name and firm telephone number on file with TREC) may be placed no more than one click away from the viewable social media page or advertisement.
    • Example Application: If Licensee Leila posts a listing advertisement on Facebook and clicking the link takes the viewer directly to Leila's website where the brokerage's licensed name and telephone number are conspicuously displayed, Leila has satisfied Tennessee's "one click away" requirement.
  • Cybercrime & Data Retention Risks in Real Estate
    • Why Brokerages Are Prime Targets: Because state license laws require brokerages to retain transaction records for several years, firms routinely store physical and digital files containing highly sensitive client personally identifiable information (PII), including:
      • Bank account information
      • Credit card numbers
      • Contact information
      • Social Security numbers
      • Driver's license numbers
    • Brokerage & Licensee Liability: If confidential client data is compromised or stolen due to lax security practices (such as weak passwords or unencrypted transmissions), both the individual licensee and the brokerage can be held legally and financially liable.
  • Wire Fraud & Legal Liability
    • How Wire Fraud Happens: Cybercriminals compromise an email account (such as a seller's, agent's, or title company's account) and send authentic-looking emails instructing the buyer to wire closing funds to a fraudulent account.
    • Court Precedent on Agent Liability: Real estate licensees are not automatically immune when a client falls victim to wire fraud—for example, a state appellate court upheld a verdict holding a seller's agent 85% liable for a buyer's wire fraud loss after the broker forwarded a fraudulent email containing altered wire instructions.
    • Wire Fraud Prevention Measures:
      • Avoid unsecured networks: Never transmit unencrypted, confidential financial data over free or public wireless networks.
      • Mandate verbal out-of-band verification: Instruct clients to contact wire recipients by phone to confirm wiring instructions immediately before sending funds.
      • Use independently verified contact details: Verify phone numbers and web addresses using trusted, pre-established sources rather than links or phone numbers embedded inside an email.
      • Practice inbox hygiene: Regularly clean out email inboxes so sensitive financial documents and client records are not sitting exposed.
      • Enforce strong password & network hygiene: Use strong passwords, change them regularly, enable multi-factor authentication, and keep network security software and firewalls updated.
      • Include an email signature disclaimer: Add a prominent wire fraud warning/disclaimer to your email signature block.
  • Identity Theft & Data Breach Liability
    • Consequences of Compromised Client Data: If a hacker breaches a brokerage's system and steals client financial records (such as credit card or bank account numbers) to commit identity theft, the client can sue.
    • Scenario Example (Licensee Lou): When investigators determine a breach occurred because Licensee Lou used a weak password to store confidential client records, the brokerage faces liability for attorney's fees, court costs, and client damages.
  • Computer, Phone & Social Media Security Best Practices
    Security AreaRecommended Best Practice
    Email & Forwarding CautionDouble-check recipient addresses before forwarding emails, texts, or client documents to prevent accidental disclosure of confidential data.
    Phishing & Malware PreventionNever click on links or attachments from unknown or unverified senders; maintain and regularly update antivirus and security software.
    Device & Physical SecurityAlways lock your computer screen whenever stepping away from your workstation, and keep your mobile phone on your person to protect it from theft just like a computer.
    Data BackupsRegularly back up critical transaction files using an encrypted external drive or secure cloud backup system so data can be recovered after a system failure or attack.

    Social Media ProfessionalismTreat all social media posts as public-facing marketing—never disparage competing agents and never discuss commission rates (which could be construed as illegal price fixing).

Key Concepts & Principles

  • National Advertising Laws
    • The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) regulates telephone solicitations. The most important component of the TCPA is the Do Not Call Registry, which regulates telemarketing calls from commercial organizations (such as brokerages).
    • The CAN-SPAM Act was created to govern the use of commercial email advertising.
    • The Junk Fax Prevention Act governs the use of fax advertisements.
  • Tennessee Online Advertising Guidelines
    • Every web page needs to conspicuously show the brokerage's name and telephone number.
    • Every web page that shows property listings from an outside database needs to state that the listings may not belong to the brokerage.
    • Every ad created by a brokerage (known as first-generation advertising) needs to be accurate and up-to-date. A licensee is not responsible, though, for the accuracy of syndicated ads or third-party listings.
    • These advertising guidelines also apply to social media. Here, though, required info (i.e., the brokerage name and telephone number) can be one click away from the ad.
  • Tennessee Legislation
    • The Tennessee Consumer Protection Act (TCPTA): Passed in 1977, it prohibits unfair and deceptive trade practices.
    • Tennessee Do Not Call Program: This is similar to federal law.
