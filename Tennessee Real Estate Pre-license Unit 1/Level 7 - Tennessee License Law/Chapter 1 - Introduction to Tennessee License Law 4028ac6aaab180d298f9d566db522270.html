Chapter 1 - Introduction to Tennessee License Law

Purpose & Value of Real Estate License Laws

  • Consumer Protection: The fundamental purpose of any state real estate license law is to safeguard members of the public—including buyers, sellers, tenants, and property owners—from harmful or unethical actions by licensees.
  • Standardization: Establishes uniform processes, professional expectations, and clear operational standards within the real estate industry to improve transaction outcomes for all parties.

The Tennessee Real Estate Broker License Act of 1973

  • Enactment & Statute: Established in 1973, Tennessee's governing license law is officially titled the Tennessee Real Estate Broker License Act.
  • Legal Code Location: Codified under Chapter 13, Title 62 of the Tennessee Code Annotated (T.C.A.).
  • Core Purpose: Created to regulate real estate professionals and protect the general public against misconduct or harmful practices.
  • Main Elements of Tennessee License Law
    • Outlines basic qualifications and ongoing requirements for obtaining and maintaining a real estate license.
    • Clearly distinguishes between activities that legally require a license and those that do not.
    • Sets mandatory professional standards of conduct and practice.
    • Establishes legal consequences, disciplinary actions, and penalties for violating regulatory standards.
    • Governs educational providers and pre-licensing/continuing education rules.
    • Provides the statutory authority for creating and empowering the Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC).
  • The Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC) Overview
    • Role: The official regulatory body established to oversee the real estate profession in Tennessee.
    • Primary Goals:
    • Protect the public's interest in both residential and commercial real estate transactions.
    • Establish mandatory education and pre-licensing standards for licensees.
    • Strictly enforce Tennessee's real estate license laws.
  • Regulatory Authority & Hierarchy
    • Statutory Foundation: State legislators create and pass statutes (such as the Tennessee Real Estate Broker License Act of 1973).
    • Commission Rules: TREC adopts rules to promulgate (clarify and enforce) the state license law.
    • Policy Statements: TREC occasionally issues Policy Statements to further amplify or clarify its rules, which subsequently become part of the regulatory framework.
    • Distinction in Lawmaking: TREC does not possess the power to make statutory laws (a power reserved exclusively for the state legislature); instead, TREC administers, promulgates, and enforces rules and regulations.
  • TREC's Jurisdiction & Areas of Regulation
    • Licenses, registers, and regulates real estate brokers and affiliate brokers.
    • Oversees real estate firms.
    • Regulates rental location agents, timeshare salespersons and developments, vacation clubs, and vacation lodging services.
    • Composition of the Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC)
      • Total Members: 9 members, all of whom must be residents of Tennessee of good moral character.
      • Appointment & Terms: Appointed by the governor to serve five-year terms, with members prohibited from succeeding themselves for more than one full term.
    • Membership Distribution & Qualifications
      • Industry Experience: 7 members must have been licensed real estate brokers or affiliate brokers in Tennessee for at least 5 years.
      • Public Members: 2 members must represent the general public (unlicensed in real estate) and cannot reside in the same grand division.
      • Geographic Representation: Must include exactly 3 members from each of Tennessee's three grand divisions (Eastern, Middle, and Western divisions).
      • Diversity Requirements: At least 1 member must be 60 years of age or older, and at least 1 member must be a person of color.
    • Meetings & Operational Rules
      • Meeting Locations: At least one meeting must be held annually in each of the three grand divisions, with additional meetings scheduled as necessary.
      • Quorum Requirements: A minimum of 5 members must be in attendance for a TREC meeting to be valid.
      • Transparency: All official meetings are open to the public.
    • Core Powers & Judicial Role
      • Quasi-Judicial Authority: TREC holds the legal power to determine whether a violation of license law has occurred.
      • Civil Immunity: Commission members enjoy immunity from civil liability when performing their official duties in good faith.
      • Legal Representation: Members have the right to be represented by the state's Attorney General.
    • Internal Organization & Administration
      • Leadership: Members elect a chairperson and vice chairperson from among themselves.
      • Official Seal: Authorized to use an official seal to authenticate official records and documents.
      • Meetings: Empowered to hold necessary meetings, with the mandatory requirement of at least one meeting per year in each of Tennessee's three grand divisions.
    • Executive & Professional Appointments
      • Executive Director: Appointed to a four-year term (subject to approval by the Commissioner of Personnel). Must have passed the Tennessee broker's exam and meet specific qualifications set by TREC.
      • Director of Education: Required to hold a college degree from an accredited university.
      • General Counsel: Provided with a dedicated, full-time licensed attorney well-versed in Tennessee law.
    • Regulatory & Financial Powers
      • Rulemaking: Authorized to adopt bylaws, rules, and regulations to enforce license law, provided they do not contradict Tennessee statutes.
      • Recovery Account Administration: Manages and administers the Real Estate Education and Recovery Account, which provides compensation to consumers who win a civil lawsuit against a licensee but are unable to collect the awarded damages.
    • Practical Day-to-Day Functions of TREC
      • Licensing Management: Reviewing, approving, or denying initial license applications, as well as managing renewals, suspensions, and revocations.
      • Fee Adjustments: Authority to increase licensing fees as necessary to support administrative operations.
      • Educational Oversight: Approving real estate schools, licensing exam test vendors, and promulgating rules governing pre-licensing courses and continuing education (CE).
      • Requirements & Waivers: Empowered to waive specific examination or education requirements under qualifying circumstances.
      • Industry Support & Training: Conducting or assisting in real estate clinics, educational meetings, specialized courses, and professional institutes.
    • Real Estate Education and Recovery Account Overview
      • Purpose: A consumer protection fund administered by TREC to reimburse clients or customers who win a civil judgment against a licensee for misconduct (such as fraud, misrepresentation, or deceit) but are unable to collect the damages owed.
      • Application Timeline: If a court judgment remains unpaid for 60 days, the aggrieved party may formally apply to TREC for relief from the fund.
    • Funding & Minimum Balance Requirements
      • Minimum Balance: The account must maintain a minimum statutory balance of $500,000.
      • Source of Funds: Funded primarily through fees collected from applicants seeking broker, affiliate broker, or timeshare salesperson licenses.
      • Assessments: If the fund balance drops below $500,000, TREC can levy an additional assessment of up to $30 upon all active licensees at the time of license renewal.
    • Payout Caps & Limitations on Liability
      • Per Transaction Limit: The maximum payout a consumer can receive from the account for a single transaction is $15,000.
      • Per Licensee Limit: The absolute maximum liability payout from the fund for a single licensee is $30,000, regardless of how many individual victims file claims or how many separate transactions are involved.
      • Exclusions: Payouts cover actual damages awarded by a judge; they typically do not cover attorney fees or punitive damages if they exceed statutory limits.
    • Consequences for the Licensee
      • If any money is paid out from the Recovery Account on behalf of a licensee, TREC holds the discretionary authority to suspend or revoke that individual's real estate license.

Key Terms

  • Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC) - group that regulates the real estate industry in Tennessee in order to protect the public's interest; aka TREC or the Commission
  • Real Estate Education and Recovery Fund - fund serving the purpose of reimbursing people who have been harmed by a licensee's conduct

Key Concepts & Principles

  • The Tennessee Real Estate Broker License Act of 1973
    • The license law which real estate professionals must follow in Tennessee is contained in Chapter 13, Title 62 of the Tennessee Code Annotated. This law standardizes processes and expectations within the real estate industry, including:
      • Basic requirements for licensure and maintaining a license
      • Distinctions between acts that require a license and acts that do not
      • Standards of conduct and practice
      • Consequences for violating those standards
      • Regulations for education providers (like Aceable!)
      • Creation of the Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC)
  • The Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC)
    • To regulate the real estate industry in order to protect the public's interest when involved in real estate transactions
    • To establish standards for the education of licensees
    • To enforce Tennessee's license law
    • What TREC CANNOT Do
      • TREC is NOT allowed to set fees or commissions for licensees! They will not take commission complaints, either.
