Chapter 3 - Responsibilities in the Relationships

Agency Relationships & Common Law Overview

  • Origin of Rules: Many professional real estate rules and regulations originate from the common law of agency.
  • Standard of Care Hierarchy: Common law establishes that significantly higher-level duties—known as fiduciary duties—are owed exclusively to an agent's principal (client), as opposed to customers and third parties where no agency relationship exists.
  • The Duty of Disclosure Overview
    • Overriding Duty: Disclosure is a foundational, overriding duty in the law of agency that reappears throughout real estate practice.
    • The Two Primary Categories: Real estate disclosures generally fall into one of two distinct types:
    1. Agency disclosure (revealing representation status, who the licensee represents, and when that representation begins or changes).
    1. Disclosure of material facts (revealing physical, legal, or financial facts about the property or transaction that could impact a party's decision-making).
  • Agency Disclosure
    • Definition: The process by which a licensee reveals potential or existing agency relationships and interests to parties in a transaction.
    • Core Requirement: Unrepresented prospects must be educated on available agency options (keeping in mind that without a written agreement, licensees default to neutral facilitators/non-agents). Existing relationships must be disclosed to opposing parties (buyers to sellers, sellers to buyers) and to other cooperating licensees involved in the transaction.
    • First Substantive Contact
      • Trigger Point: The critical legal threshold requiring agency disclosure.
      • Timing: Typically occurs at the first face-to-face meeting with a customer and prior to:
        • Sharing any confidential information.
        • Discussing or taking any action regarding a specific property.
    • Informed Consent
      • Definition: The legal principle ensuring that clients and customers fully understand and are aware of actions or agency relationships before implementation.
      • Connection to Contract Law: Shares roots with "reality of consent" (a contract validity requirement)—parties cannot genuinely consent without having all relevant facts at hand regarding representation.
    • Tennessee Disclosure Timing Rules
      • General Timing: Must occur (verbally or in writing) before providing any real estate services.
      • Mandatory Written Disclosure: Must be executed in writing before a licensee:
        • Enters into a representation agreement with a buyer or seller.
        • Assists in the creation or presentation of an offer to buy.
  • Agency Disclosure Forms
    • Requirement: Because Tennessee law requires agency disclosures to be in writing, licensees must use proper disclosure forms.
    • TREC Position: The Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC) does not promulgate a mandatory form for agency disclosure.
    • Tennessee REALTORS® Association Forms: Members commonly use two standard forms provided by the association:
    • RF301 (Working with a Real Estate Professional): Tailored for use with unrepresented parties prior to establishing an agency relationship.
    • RF302 (Confirmation of Agency Status): Designed to inform all interested parties of an existing agency relationship.
    • Deep Dive: RF302 (Confirmation of Agency Status)
      • When Used: Typically presented before:
        1. The preparation or presentation of an offer to purchase.
        1. The execution of a listing agreement (if listing a property without a prior agency agreement).
      • Information Disclosed:
      • Identifies all individuals involved (Buyer, Seller, Licensee(s)).
      • Specifies existing agency or non-agency relationships for each party (Transaction broker/facilitator, buyer/seller agent, designated agent, dual agent, or unrepresented status).
      • Signatures Required:
      • Obtains signatures from the buyer or seller.
      • Crucial Distinction: Signing does not commit the party to an agency relationship; it merely serves as legal proof that the disclosure was provided in a timely manner.
      • Applicability: Applies equally to landlords and tenants in lease transactions.

  • Agency Disclosure: Conflicts of Interest
    • Core Requirement: Disclosing potential conflicts of interest is mandated by state law and the National Association of REALTORS® (NAR) Code of Ethics. Licensees must maintain total transparency and make disclosures to both clients and customers as soon as they become aware of a conflict.
    • Severe Consequences: Failure to disclose personal or other conflicts of interest can lead to the suspension or revocation of a real estate license.
    • Key Disclosures Required
      • Ownership Interest: Any current or desired future ownership interest in the subject property.
      • Personal Relationships: Any familial, friendly, or business/organizational relationship with either principal in the transaction.
      • Financial Compensations: Receipt of any referral fees or rebates.
      • Impairment of Duties: Any other material fact that could interfere with the licensee's ability to fulfill their duties to all parties.
    • Documentation & Forms
      • Requirement: Personal interests and conflicts must be disclosed in writing.
      • Tennessee REALTORS® Form: Members utilize a specific Tennessee REALTORS® association form to document personal interests, ensuring all parties sign to acknowledge and verify receipt of the disclosure.
  • Agency Disclosure Recap
    • Primary Purpose: To provide vital information before entering into agency relationships and to explicitly make known any existing agency relationships or personal conflicts of interest.
    • Target Audiences for Disclosure:
      • Unrepresented parties (to educate them on available options and default non-agency status).
      • Represented opposing parties.
      • Other licensees involved in the same prospective transaction.
    • Required Documentation in Tennessee: Agency disclosure and status confirmation must be formally communicated using the Confirmation of Agency Status form (such as Tennessee REALTORS® Form RF302).
  • Disclosure of Material Facts
    • Definition: A material fact (frequently referred to in Tennessee as an adverse fact) is any piece of information that, if known, could cause a buyer or seller to take a different course of action or make a different decision regarding the property purchase.
    • Core Focus: Discussions heavily center on the physical, legal, and structural condition of the subject property, as material facts regarding property matters carry the greatest weight in a transaction.
    • Purpose & Role in Transactions
      • Informed Consent: Disclosing material facts regarding property condition is essential for creating a transparent contractual environment that allows the buyer to make fully informed decisions.
      • Agent Utilization: While sellers complete property condition disclosure forms, listing agents rely on these documents (with the seller's assistance) to identify and understand critical material facts that must be shared with prospective buyers.
  • Disclosure of Financial Condition or Motivation
    • Core Principle: A party's financial condition can be a material fact, but its disclosure depends entirely on whether it impacts transaction performance versus confidential negotiations.
    • Financial Information to Hold Confidential
      • Negotiation Leverage Details: Information regarding a party's financial strength or underlying personal motivation must be kept strictly confidential if disclosing it would weaken their bargaining position.
      • Examples:
        • A buyer having ample funds well beyond the purchase price.
        • A seller needing a rapid sale due to accepting an out-of-state job relocation.
    • Financial Information to Disclose (Ability to Perform)
      • Transaction Viability: Any financial condition that casts real doubt on a party's ability to fulfill their contractual obligations—or confirms their capability to proceed—must be shared.
      • Examples:
        • Confirmation that a buyer has secured or has the clear ability to obtain the necessary financing.
        • Confirmation that a seller holds a clean, marketable title to the property.
  • Agent Duties Overview
    • Hierarchy of Duties: The remainder of the chapter contrasts the higher-level fiduciary duties owed exclusively to clients with the lesser standard of duties owed to customers and third parties.
    • Broker-Level Obligation: Because agency is established at the broker level, every affiliate broker sponsored by that principal broker is legally bound to uphold the exact same duties to the principal as the broker who executed the agreement.
  • Fiduciary Duties Overview
    • Definition: Fiduciary duties represent the full sum of obligations owed exclusively to a client (principal) in an agency relationship.
    • Standard of Conduct: A fiduciary must always act in the best interest of the client, placing the client's interests above all others—including the agent's own personal interests.
    • High Trust Relationship: Real estate agents are classified as fiduciaries due to the high degree of trust and confidence required in the principal-agent relationship.
    • The Acronym (OLDCAR): While specific terminology may vary slightly across states, the standard fiduciary duties are universally remembered and categorized under the acronym OLDCAR.
  • Fiduciary Duty: Obedience
    • Decision-Makers: Clients are the ultimate decision-makers in real estate transactions, not agents.
      • Sellers decide listing prices and whether to accept offers.
      • Buyers decide how much to offer.
    • Agent's Role: Agents provide professional advice and guidance, but their core duty is to obey their clients' lawful instructions.
    • The Limitation: Unlawful or Unethical Instructions
      • Rule of Law & Ethics: The duty of obedience is strictly limited—obedience is never required if the principal's instructions are illegal or unethical in any way. Agents must refuse any directive that violates real estate laws, fair housing regulations, or ethical codes.
  • Fiduciary Duty: Loyalty
    • Definition: The agent has a binding fiduciary duty of loyalty to their principal, requiring them to champion and protect the principal's best interests above all others, including the agent's own personal or financial interests.
    • Professional Integrity: While licensees may occasionally face temptations or opportunities to prioritize personal gain over a client's welfare, strict adherence to loyalty requires putting the client first in every transaction decision.
  • Fiduciary Duty: Disclosure
    • Definition: The agent has a binding fiduciary duty to disclose to their principal all material facts related to the transaction that would be beneficial or relevant to the principal.
    • Scope of Knowledge: Because agents are fiduciaries, this duty covers not only facts the agent actually knows, but also facts the agent should have known (representing the intersection of disclosure and reasonable care).
    • Examples of Disclosable Facts
      • Property physical condition and value.
      • Environmental hazards (e.g., presence of radon in soil).
      • Encroachments, zoning restrictions, and tax details.
      • Opposing party's motivation or financial standing (if legally permissible and known).
    • The Golden Rule of Disclosure
      • If it is information you would want to know if you were in your client's shoes, the duty of full disclosure requires you to share it.
  • Fiduciary Duty: Confidentiality
    • Definition: Closely tied to the duty of loyalty, confidentiality requires that private information shared by a client must be kept strictly secret unless the client grants explicit permission to share it.
    • Listing Agent Context: Sellers may reveal bottom-line pricing or urgent selling motivations during listing presentations; sharing this would weaken their negotiating leverage.
    • Buyer's Agent Context: Buyers may disclose their absolute maximum price or strict relocation timelines while drafting offers; sharing this would harm their negotiating position.
    • Unrepresented Parties: Information of a confidential nature learned from an unrepresented party before agency disclosure must also be held in confidence unless released by that party.
    • Duration: Confidentiality Is Forever
      • Survival Beyond Contract: Unlike most agency duties that end when the transaction closes or the representation agreement terminates, the duty of confidentiality survives forever, even if the relationship ends on poor terms.
    • Exceptions to Confidentiality
      • Licensees are legally required or permitted to break confidentiality under specific circumstances:
        • Material facts regarding the condition of the property.
        • Information demanded under a court order or subpoena.
        • Information the client specifically instructs the agent to share (always get client instructions to share confidential info in writing).
  • Fiduciary Duty: Accounting
    • Definition: The obligation of a licensee to properly handle, safeguard, and account for all funds and financial matters entrusted to them by a client.
    • Core Prohibition (Commingling): Licensees are strictly prohibited from using client funds for personal use or storing them in personal accounts. Mixing client funds with personal funds is known as commingling and is illegal.
    • Record-Keeping: Extends to maintaining accurate, state-compliant financial records and documentation related to the transaction.
    • Duration: Another Forever Duty
      • Just like confidentiality, the fiduciary duty of accounting survives the termination of the agency relationship.
  • Fiduciary Duty: Reasonable Skill and Care
    • Definition: The obligation of a licensee to provide a high standard of professional competence and expertise on behalf of their client, bridging the knowledge gap between the professional agent and the consumer.
    • Core Principle: Clients hire real estate professionals trusting they possess the specialized knowledge and skills required to navigate the transaction successfully.
    • Scope of Application
      • Licensees must exercise reasonable skill and care across all operational activities, including:
        • Filling out legal paperwork and drafting purchase offers.
        • Entering accurate information into the Multiple Listing Service (MLS).
        • Preparing marketing materials, property photos, and advertisements.
        • Staging and showing properties.
        • Negotiating sales prices and contract terms.
      • Legal Liability & Competency
        • Standard of Competency: This duty ties directly into the duty of disclosure—agents are legally held responsible not only for what they actually know, but also for what they should have known given their professional standing.
        • Consequences of Incompetence: Licensees can be held legally and professionally liable for failing to exercise the expected level of expertise associated with their position.
  • Duties to Customers and Third Parties Overview
    • Core Principle: Because no agency relationship exists with customers and third parties, agents do not owe them fiduciary duties (such as loyalty or obedience), but are still bound by a specific set of standard duties under common and statutory law.
    • The Four Key Duties Owed:
      1. Disclosure (agency status and material/adverse facts).
      1. Honesty and fair dealing.
      1. Reasonable skill and care.
      1. Accounting (when handling funds).
    • Deep Dive: The Four Non-Client Duties
      • Disclosure:
        • Required: Must disclose who they represent (agency disclosure) and share all material/adverse facts regarding property condition and a party's ability to perform.
        • Excluded: Full disclosure of confidential client information (such as bottom-line pricing or personal motivations) is strictly prohibited to protect the client's negotiating position.
      • Honesty and Fair Dealing: Mandates that licensees act with integrity and completely avoid deceptive, fraudulent, or misleading behavior in all interactions.
      • Reasonable Skill and Care: Requires agents to meet the professional standards and competency expected of licensed real estate professionals across all dealings, even with non-clients.
      • Accounting: While less frequent with non-clients, if an agent holds money or escrow funds for a customer, they are held to the exact same strict accountability standards as they are for a client.
    • Ministerial Acts
      • Definition: Non-discretionary, administrative tasks performed to keep a transaction moving forward.
      • Compatibility: Agents can freely perform ministerial acts for customers (e.g., presenting offers, handling documents) without violating or conflicting with the fiduciary duties owed to their own clients.
  • Client & Customer Duties: Recap
  • Agent Duties to the Sponsoring Broker
    • General Agency Relationship: An affiliate broker acts as an agent for their sponsoring (principal) broker in a general agency relationship.
    • Fiduciary Obligations: Affiliate brokers owe fiduciary duties directly to their sponsoring broker.
    • Fulfilling the Duty: The most effective way for an affiliate broker to fulfill their fiduciary duties to their sponsoring broker is by faithfully helping the broker uphold the fiduciary duties owed to their clients.
  • Duties a Principal Owes to the Agent
    • Mutual Obligation: While the primary focus is on agent responsibilities, principals also owe distinct legal duties to their agents under an agency relationship.
    • The Four Principal Duties: Cooperation, Compensation, Information, and Indemnification.
  • Deep Dive: The Four Principal Duties
    • Cooperation: Because agents operate under special agency without the power to bind the principal, the principal must actively cooperate by being available to sign documents, providing direction, and ensuring the property is accessible for showings to support the agent's efforts.
    • Compensation:
      • The agency agreement must clearly specify how compensation is earned and when it is due.
      • Procuring Cause Rule: A listing agreement is an employment contract to procure a ready, willing, and able buyer, not a contract to sell the property. If an agent produces such a buyer making a full-price, unconditional offer, the agent has earned their commission—even if the seller ultimately refuses to accept the offer.
    • Information: The principal must be forthcoming with all critical data needed to complete the transaction (e.g., sellers providing accurate property disclosures revealing latent defects; buyers providing accurate financial profiles).
    • Indemnification: The principal's obligation to protect the agent from liability when acting on the principal's behalf. Listing agreements typically contain an indemnification clause where the seller agrees to hold the agent harmless for losses or damages, except for those arising from the agent's own gross negligence or willful misconduct.

Key Terms

  • fiduciary duties - the full sum of obligations owed to the client in an agency relationship that ensures that the agent always acts in the best interest of the client
  • first substantive contact - the critical point at which agency disclosure is required by a license holder; generally occurs at the first face-to-face meeting with the customer and prior to the sharing of any confidential information or initiation of any action in regards to a specific property
  • informed consent - the principle that a client or customer must understand and agree to certain actions or agency relationships prior to implementation

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Agency Disclosure - happens when a licensee makes known possible or existing agency relationships/interests.
    • The two primary types of disclosures are:
      • Agency disclosure
      • Disclosure of material facts
    • In Tennessee, agency disclosure should be documented with the Confirmation of Agency Status form.
    • Disclosure of potential conflicts of interest is a subset of agency disclosures.
  • Disclosure of Material Facts - is a key component of the general duty of disclosure and the common law of agency. A material fact is one that, if known, could cause a buyer or seller of real estate to take a different course of action or make a different decision regarding the property purchase.
    • In Tennessee, these kinds of material facts are often referred to as adverse facts.
  • Disclosure of Financial Condition - If a party's financial condition casts real doubt on their ability to perform in the purchase contract, it's a material fact that should be shared. Otherwise, financial condition and motivation should be held in confidence.
  • Fiduciary Duties - A fiduciary always acts in the best interest of the client. Often, these duties are taught (and remembered!) by the acronym OLDCAR.
  • Duties to Customers and Third Parties
    • The lesser duties owed to customers and third parties are:
      • Disclosure
      • Honesty and fair dealing
      • Reasonable skill and care
      • Accounting
  • Agent Duties to the Sponsoring Broker
    • An affiliate broker is an agent for their sponsoring broker in a general agency relationship. And, as such, they have fiduciary duties to uphold.
  • Duties a Principal Owes to the Agent
    • Cooperation
    • Compensation
    • Information
    • Indemnification
