Chapter 3 - Filling Out the Contract

Completing Contracts vs. Unauthorized Practice of Law (T.C.A. § 62-13-312(b)(19))

  • Color Inside the Lines: Tennessee real estate licensees are permitted to fill in factual business blanks on attorney-drafted, preprinted brokerage or Tennessee REALTORS® forms (Form RF401 and related addenda/amendments). Creating an original contract or drafting complex legal clauses crosses into the unauthorized practice of law, which is grounds for license discipline, suspension, or revocation.
  • Five Best Practices for Completing Contracts:
    1. Never strike through preprinted contract language: If terms need modification, use an official addendum, amendment, or the Special Stipulations section rather than crossing out preprinted provisions.
    1. Fill in every blank (N/A): Enter "N/A" (not applicable) in any unused field so blank spaces cannot be altered later.
    1. Never charge a separate fee for completing contracts: Charging a document-preparation fee constitutes practicing law without a license.
    1. Preserve preprinted legal meanings: Do not insert unusual stipulations that distort the form's legal structure or attempt to address complex legal scenarios the form was not designed to cover.
    1. Refer legal questions to an attorney: Always recommend that clients consult a licensed Tennessee real estate attorney whenever they have legal questions, want custom legal language, or are unsure of their legal rights.
  • "Explain, Don't Advise":
    • Permitted (Explaining): Read preprinted contract provisions with clients, clarify factual business terms, and highlight critical deadlines so they understand what they are signing.
    • Prohibited (Advising): Tell clients what legal choice they should make or interpret how a contract clause will play out legally.
  • Core Components of a Real Estate Purchase Contract (Tennessee REALTORS® Form RF401)
    • Mandatory Legal Requirements (Must Be Present for Validity & Enforceability):
      • Full legal names of the parties: Specifically the buyer(s) and seller(s) (note that real estate licensees and brokerages are not parties to the purchase contract).
      • Legal description of the property: A street address alone is insufficient—the contract must identify the property by its recorded legal description (county, deed book/page or instrument number, and/or lot/subdivision).
      • Signatures of all bound parties: Signed and dated by all buyers and sellers in compliance with the Statute of Frauds.
      • Evidence of intent to convey ownership: Explicit words of conveyance stating that the seller agrees to sell and the buyer agrees to buy an ownership interest in the real property.
      • Terms of the sale & purchase price: The exact purchase price, method of payment (cash vs. financing), and material conditions of transfer.
    • Standard Operational Sections Typically Included in Form RF401:
      • Financing / mortgage details: Loan type (Conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, or all-cash), loan contingency deadlines, and good-faith application duties.
      • Earnest money / trust money deposit: Deposit amount, holder (escrow/trustee), delivery deadline, and handling/disbursement rules.
      • Closing date, location & possession: Target closing date, title/closing agent designation, prorations (taxes, HOA dues, rents), and possession timing.
      • Inclusions & exclusions: Specific fixtures and built-in items included with the property (e.g., ceiling fans, attached lighting, built-in appliances, window treatments) versus excluded items or leased equipment.
      • Inspection & appraisal contingencies: Inspection timeframe, repair negotiation/resolution procedures, and appraisal value contingency.
      • Acceptance procedures, deadlines & disclosures: Offer expiration deadline (Time is of the Essence), Binding Agreement Date mechanics, mandatory property disclosures (Residential Property Condition Disclosure, Lead-Based Paint), and wire-fraud/agency confirmations.

  • Special Stipulations ("Special Stips") & Tennessee Form RF707
    • Purpose & Hierarchy Rule (Handwritten > Typed > Preprinted): Special stipulations add transaction-specific conditions not already in the form or modify existing preprinted terms. Under contract interpretation rules, handwritten language overrides typed language, and typed language overrides preprinted contract language—meaning anything written in Special Stipulations can supersede conflicting preprinted terms in the contract.
    • Best Practices & Risk Management:
      • Use approved forms first: Never write a custom stipulation if a preprinted Tennessee REALTORS® form or addendum already covers the contingency or issue.
      • Use Tennessee REALTORS® Form RF707 (Additional Contract Language / Special Stipulations Library): Copy pre-drafted, attorney-approved clauses from RF707 (covering items such as septic/well certifications, radon remediation, buyer/agent investor intent, or survey contingencies) rather than inventing legal language.
      • Be specific and unambiguous: State exact dollar amounts, calendar dates, and responsible parties. Never enter vague placeholders like "TBD", "actual costs", or "negotiable", which can render a provision unenforceable.
      • Refer complex drafting to an attorney: Consult your principal broker or advise the client to hire a Tennessee real estate attorney whenever custom legal wording is needed so you do not commit the unauthorized practice of law.

  • Definition & Legal Effect of Contingencies
    • What a Contingency Is: A contractual provision making the parties' obligation to close conditional upon the occurrence or satisfaction of a specified event.
    • Legal Status While Active: Until contingencies are satisfied or waived in writing, the contract remains conditionally voidable by the protected party without breach or forfeiture of earnest money—provided they act in good faith and deliver timely written notice before the deadline.
  • Five Common Real Estate Contingencies & Tennessee RF401 Rules
    Contingency TypeCore Purpose & ProtectionTennessee REALTORS® Form RF401 Deadlines & Mechanics

Financing (Mortgage) ContingencyProtects the buyer from being obligated to buy if they cannot secure adequate mortgage financing under customary underwriting criteria.• If Financing Contingency Applies: Buyer must apply for the loan and pay for the credit report within 3 days after the Binding Agreement Date (BAD), and within 14 days after BAD warrant via written notification (Form RF656) that they have notified the lender of Intent to Proceed, secured hazard insurance, and have sufficient funds to close.

    • If Financing Contingency Is Waived (e.g., All-Cash): Buyer must provide proof of available funds to close within 5 days after BAD, and cannot use loan denial to cancel or recover earnest money.
    2. Appraisal ContingencyProtects the buyer from overpaying if the property appraises below the agreed purchase price.• If contingent on appraisal, buyer must order the appraisal and furnish the appraiser's name/phone and proof of order within 5 days after BAD.

    • If the appraisal comes in below purchase price, the buyer must deliver written notice within the contract's appraisal deadline to either terminate (and recover earnest money) or waive the contingency; failure to act on time waives the contingency automatically.

  • Termination Mechanics & Preventing Contingency Abuse
    • Strict Communication Rules: Termination under a contingency is never automatic—the terminating party must deliver formal written notice (such as Tennessee REALTORS® Notification Form RF656 and a Trust Money Disbursement and Release form) in strict compliance with the contract's notice provisions before the contingency deadline expires.
    • Three Safeguards Against Contingency Abuse: To prevent either party from using a contingency as a bad-faith "free exit," every contingency clause must:
      1. State clear, objective criteria specifying exactly how the condition is satisfied, waived, or invoked.
      1. Include a firm expiration date (Time is of the Essence) after which the contingency is waived or terminated.
      1. Explicitly require good-faith diligence and effort to fulfill the obligation (e.g., applying for a loan within 3 days and pursuing underwriting in good faith rather than dragging feet to engineer a cancellation).
  • Contract-to-Closing Responsibilities & Risk Management (TREC Rule 1260-02-.08 & Form RF401)
    • 1. Maintain Open Communication:
      • Keep active, documented communication flowing among the buyer, seller, cooperating licensee, lender, and title company or closing attorney so issues surface early rather than at the closing table.
    • 2. Distribute Executed Contract Copies Immediately (TREC Rule 1260-02-.08):
      • Promptly deliver true, fully signed copies of the bound purchase contract (and all addenda/counteroffers) to the buyer, seller, sponsoring principal brokers, mortgage lender, and title company/closing attorney.
    • 3. Track Earnest Money & "Time Is of the Essence" Deadlines:
      • Verify that the earnest money/trust money deposit is delivered to the designated Holder by the contract deadline (under RF401, if earnest money is not delivered on time or a check bounces, the Holder notifies both parties and the buyer has 1 day to cure with immediately available funds before the seller may terminate for default).
      • Actively calendar and remind clients of every "Time Is of the Essence" deadline—especially the Inspection Period, Resolution Period, Appraisal deadline, and Financing milestones. If a buyer discovers termites or major defects during inspection but waits until after the inspection deadline expires to deliver written termination notice (Form RF656), they lose their contingency exit and risk defaulting and forfeiting their earnest money.
    • 4. Monitor Loan Processing, Appraisal & Title Progress:
      • Follow up regularly with the buyer's loan officer to confirm timely loan application (3 days under RF401), appraisal ordering (5 days under RF401), 14day financing/intent-to-proceed verification, underwriting conditions, title commitment clearance, and issuance of the lender's Clear to Close.
    • 5. Prepare Clients for Closing & Pre-Review Documents:
      • Walk clients through what to expect at the closing table (whether conducted by a title company or closing attorney in Tennessee), coordinate the final walk-through, remind buyers of wire-fraud verification protocols and valid government-issued photo IDs, and ensure they review closing documents prior to closing day (including the lender's Closing Disclosure, which federal TRID rules require the buyer to receive at least 3 business days before consummation).
    • 6. Provide Updated Estimated Cost & Net Worksheets:
      • Supply buyers with an updated Estimated Closing Cost Worksheet and sellers with an updated Estimated Seller Net Sheet ahead of closing so neither party is surprised by prorations, transfer taxes ($0.37 per $100 in Tennessee, customarily paid by the buyer), loan payoffs, or cash-to-close figures.
    • 7. Post-Closing Record Retention (T.C.A. § 62-13-321 & TREC Rule 1260-02-.40):
      • Ensure all signed contracts, disclosures, amendments, earnest money receipts, and closing statements are submitted to your principal broker to be preserved (physically or electronically) for at least 3 years following closing.

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Contract Best Practices
    • As you get used to filling out contracts, you'll get a feel for what's okay and what's not. It can be tricky, especially when it comes to the special stipulations portion of the contract. But there are some best practices that can help you:
      • Don't strike through any of the preprinted language of a contract. If you need to make changes, use an addendum or the special stipulations portion.
      • Make sure to fill in every blank, using "N/A" for fields that are not applicable.
      • You may not charge a fee for completing contracts. That could be considered practicing law.
      • Avoid changing the meaning of preprinted sections or adding unusual information that the form is not designed for.
      • Encourage clients to have a lawyer review contracts if they have any concerns. If you are unsure of how to fill out a contract, it's wise to have your client seek the advice of their attorney.
