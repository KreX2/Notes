Chapter 4 - Agency Disclosures

Three Core Agency Disclosure Scenarios (T.C.A. § 62-13-405)

  • Agency disclosure occurs when a real estate licensee makes known existing or potential agency relationships and personal interests in a transaction:
    1. With Unrepresented Prospects: Educating unrepresented consumers about available representation options (seller agency, buyer agency, designated agency, dual agency, or facilitator) and clarifying that until a written bilateral agency agreement is signed, the licensee acts solely as a facilitator (non-agency). (Note: A disclosure form alone is unilateral and never creates an agency relationship.)
    1. With Represented Parties & Other Licensees:
      • If representing a seller, disclosing that representation to prospective buyers from the outset.
      • If representing a buyer, disclosing that representation to any sellers met.
      • If representing either party, disclosing that agency status upon initial contact with any other licensee involved in the same prospective transaction—and immediately notifying all parties and licensees if that status changes later.
    1. Where There Is a Personal Interest (T.C.A. § 62-13-312(b)(18)): Disclosing in writing to all parties whenever the licensee has a personal interest beyond standard brokerage representation—including buying, selling, leasing, or holding an option on property on behalf of themselves, their immediate family, or a business entity in which they hold an ownership or financial interest.
  • First Substantive Contact & Informed Consent
    • First Substantive Contact: The critical trigger requiring agency disclosure—generally occurring at the first face-to-face meeting (or substantive phone/electronic discussion) with a consumer and prior to:
      • Sharing any confidential personal, financial, or motivational information
      • Discussing, showing, or taking action regarding a specific property
    • Informed Consent: Clients and customers cannot legally consent to an agency or non-agency arrangement unless they have all material facts beforehand. Before signing a representation agreement, a consumer must understand the duties and limitations involved; if remaining unrepresented, they must be informed of any existing agency relationships the licensee holds in the transaction.

  • Non-Promulgated Forms: The State of Tennessee (TREC) does not promulgate a single mandatory state agency disclosure form. However, whatever form a licensee uses—typically provided by their sponsoring brokerage or Tennessee REALTORS®—must include all statutory disclosures and acknowledgment elements required under T.C.A. § 62-13-405 (including TREC's address and telephone number).
  • Statutory Exemptions (T.C.A. § 62-13-405(e)): Written agency disclosure requirements under § 62-13-405 do not apply to commercial property transfers or leases, public auctions, residential properties with more than 4 units, or residential rentals/leases—though the rule that agency cannot be implied (§ 62-13-401) and baseline duties of honesty and reasonable care (§ 62-13-403) still apply.
  • Form RF301 (Working with a Real Estate Professional)
    • Non-Promulgated Status & Availability: Because the Tennessee Real Estate Commission (TREC) does not promulgate agency disclosure forms, licensees may use any form that satisfies statutory requirements (T.C.A. § 62-13-405). Form RF301 is copyrighted and provided by Tennessee REALTORS® exclusively for its members.
    • Target Audience & Timing: Designed to be presented to unrepresented consumers at first substantive contact—before confidential information is shared or specific property discussions/services take place.
    • Core Content Covered on RF301:
      • Statutory Duties Owed to All Parties (T.C.A. § 62-13-403): Explains baseline duties owed to both clients and customers (reasonable skill and care, disclosure of actual known adverse facts, pre-agency confidentiality, honesty and good faith, timely market data from public records upon request, timely accounting for funds/property, and refraining from undisclosed self-dealing or referral conflicts).
      • Additional Duties Owed to Clients (T.C.A. § 62-13-404): Explains client-level duties (obedience to lawful instructions, loyalty, scheduling showings, forwarding offers/counteroffers promptly, assisting with negotiations, and guiding post-contract closing steps).
      • Recording Devices Notice: Advises sellers and buyers regarding the potential presence and legal responsibilities surrounding audio/video recording devices on a property during showings.
      • Key Definitions & Confidentiality Rules: Defines representation roles (Facilitator/Transaction Broker, Agent for the Seller, Agent for the Buyer, Designated Agent for Seller/Buyer, Disclosed Dual Agent), adverse facts, and confidentiality vs. sharing of information—warning consumers that once a licensee discloses an agency relationship with the opposing party, any confidential information the consumer subsequently shares must be passed along to that client.
    • Signatures Required: Signed and dated by both the licensee and the unrepresented consumer to acknowledge receipt (signing RF301 does not create an agency contract).
  • Form RF302 (Confirmation of Agency Status)
    • Purpose & Scope: Provided by Tennessee REALTORS® (non-promulgated), RF302 formally documents and confirms for all interested parties the exact agency or non-agency relationships existing in a proposed sale or lease transaction (applying equally to sellers/landlords and buyers/tenants).
    • Mandatory Delivery Timing (T.C.A. § 62-13-405): Must be delivered in writing prior to:
      1. Preparing or presenting an offer to purchase (to an unrepresented buyer before preparing an offer, or to an unrepresented seller before presenting an offer), or
      1. Executing a listing agreement (if the licensee is listing a property on a non-agency/facilitator basis without an agency agreement with the seller).
    • Information Disclosed on RF302:
      • Identifies the property address, Seller/Landlord, Buyer/Tenant, and participating Licensee(s) and Firm(s).
      • Checks the exact representation status of each licensee:
        • Transaction Broker or Facilitator (not an agent for either party)
        • Agent for the Seller or Agent for the Buyer
        • Designated Agent for the Seller or Designated Agent for the Buyer
        • Disclosed Dual Agent (for both parties, with written consent of both)
        • Explicitly notes if the Seller is Unrepresented or Buyer is Unrepresented.
      • Includes statutory notice regarding filing TREC complaints (T.C.A. § 62-13-312 / § 62-13-313(e)) and confirms that compensation rates are negotiable and not set by law.
    • Effect of Signatures: Signing RF302 does not commit a buyer, seller, landlord, or tenant to an agency agreement; it serves strictly as written proof that timely agency status disclosure was provided as required by law.
  • Form RF305 (Personal Interest Disclosure & Consent)
    • Purpose & Ethical/Statutory Mandate: Both Tennessee real estate license law (T.C.A. § 62-13-312(b)(18) and § 62-13-403(7)(A)) and Article 4 / Article 5 of the NAR Code of Ethics prohibit a licensee from engaging in self-dealing or acting on behalf of themselves, their immediate family, or any business entity in which they hold an ownership or financial interest without prior written disclosure and informed consent from all parties to the transaction.
    • Role of RF305: Provided by Tennessee REALTORS®, the two-page RF305 (Personal Interest Disclosure & Consent) form serves as the standardized written notice disclosing the nature of a licensee's personal, familial, or financial interest in a property or transaction and capturing the signed written consent of all affected parties before proceeding.
      • Agency disclosure forms are not promulgated, so any form that meets state requirements can be used.
      • The purpose of this form is to disclose a licensee's personal interest in a transaction and get consent from the parties to proceed with the contract.
      • Consent must be timely, meaning it must be given BEFORE a contract is signed.
      • Direct personal interest means the licensee is participating in the transaction as the seller or buyer.
      • Indirect personal interest means the seller or buyer is an immediate family member of the licensee or is another individual, organization, or business entity in which the licensee has a personal interest.
      • Signatures on the form = acknowledgment of the disclosure and consent to proceed with the contract.
      • The RF305 is available to members of the Tennessee Realtors association.

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Agency Disclosure: What It Is
    • Agency disclosure occurs when a licensee makes known possible or existing agency relationships/interests.
    • This usually involves three scenarios:
      • When engaging with unrepresented individuals about possible agency relationships
      • When informing parties to a transaction of an existing agency relationship
      • When informing parties to a transaction of a licensee's personal interest in a transaction
  • Agency Disclosure: The Forms
    • There are three forms commonly used to disclose agency. The forms are NOT promulgated by Tennessee, so any form that meets state requirements can be used. In other words, the specific forms are not mandated, but the disclosures are.
    • The Tennessee Realtors association has three agency disclosure forms available to its membership:
      • RF301 - Working with a Real Estate Professional: used to disclose possible agency relationships to unrepresented individuals during the first substantive contact
      • RF302 - Confirmation of Agency Status: used to inform all interested parties of existing relationship a licensee has with one party to the transaction
      • RF305 - Personal Interest Disclosure & Consent: used to disclose to all interested parties of a licensee’s personal interest in the transaction
