Chapter 2 - Agent Duties in Brokerage

Brokerage Duties

  • Brokerages and their licensees carry out core professional responsibilities to ensure transactions are handled legally, ethically, and in the client's best interest.
  • These responsibilities flow through the brokerage hierarchy, keeping active agents constantly engaged in serving their clients.
  • The Core Brokerage Duties
    • 1. Fulfilling the Agent-Client Contract:
      • All contracts must be in writing.
      • Every obligation outlined in the agreement must be diligently executed.
    • 2. Seeking Out the Other Party:
      • Actively sourcing the other side of the transaction—finding a buyer for a seller's listing or locating a suitable property for a buyer client.
    • 3. Appropriate Advice:
      • Guiding clients properly within the legal scope of real estate practice and referring them to qualified experts (such as attorneys or tax advisors) when questions fall outside licensee expertise, thereby mitigating vicarious liability.
    • 4. Confidentiality:
      • Upholding the permanent fiduciary duty to protect private client information.
    • 5. Compliance:
      • Ensuring all brokerage activities, marketing, and transactions are conducted in strict accordance with federal, state, and local laws.
    • 6. Disclosure:
      • A massive, multifaceted responsibility that forms a cornerstone of real estate practice, broken down primarily into two major categories:
        1. Disclosure of Agency: Explaining and confirming who is represented (and when).
        1. Disclosure of Material Facts: Disclosing known property conditions and adverse facts critical to a decision-maker.
  • Fulfilling the Agent-Client Contract
    • Tailoring Contracts: While preprinted boiler-plate contracts (provided by brokers or local Realtor associations) serve as a standard framework, agents must customize them by filling in blanks and attaching addenda or amendments to meet each client's unique needs.
    • Matching Contract to the Prime Directive: The contract form used must align directly with the specific brokerage act being performed (Listing, Buying, Leasing, or Property Management)—never mix up contract types (e.g., using a property management contract for a buyer).
    • Staying Within the Scope (No Unauthorized Practice of Law): Utilizing approved preprinted forms keeps agents safe within their scope of practice. If a transaction requires a completely custom contract from scratch, it is time to call in a qualified real estate attorney.
    • Authority and Clarity
      • Special Agency Application: The limited authority granted through special agency is fully sufficient to help clients achieve the specific goals of the four major brokerage categories.
      • Defining Duties and Performance: Before signing, both parties must have a crystal-clear understanding of their mutual duties and what successful fulfillment looks like, with all expectations clearly spelled out in the written agreement.
  • Seeking Out the Other Party
    • The Real Estate Tango: Real estate transactions require bringing two opposing parties together (buyer/seller or landlord/tenant). As an agent, your primary mission is to help your client find the right partner to complete the dance.
    • The Bottom Line: Before you can broker a deal, you have to successfully secure and unite the parties to that deal.
    • Representing Sellers (Listing Side)
      • Staging Guidance: Advising sellers on how to present their home in its best possible light to attract buyers.
      • Pricing Strategy: Equipping sellers with up-to-date market data and information so they can establish a competitive listing price that maximizes their return without scaring off buyers.
      • Marketing Plan: Developing and executing a targeted marketing strategy to create maximum market awareness and buyer interest for the property.
    • Representing Buyers (Buy Side)
      • Affordability Analysis: Helping buyers determine a realistic budget and what they can truly afford.
      • Lender Connections: Assisting buyers in finding qualified lenders to secure pre-qualification or pre-approval for a loan.
      • Market Education: Educating buyers on current market conditions and helping them establish realistic expectations.
      • Offer Crafting: Working closely with the buyer to structure and submit a compelling, attractive purchase offer.
  • Appropriate Advice & Reasonable Care
    • The Standard of Care: Clients reasonably expect licensees to bring a higher level of professional real estate expertise and care to the relationship—which is how agents earn their commission.
    • Tennessee Legal Standard: The state explicitly holds licensees responsible not only for what they actually know, but also for what they should have known to properly guide and advise their clients.
    • The Boundary Line: Avoiding the Unauthorized Practice of Law
      • NAR Code of Ethics (Article 13): Strictly prohibits Realtors from engaging in the unauthorized practice of law and mandates that they recommend legal counsel whenever a party's interests require it. Real estate licensees practice real estate; attorneys practice law.
      • Safe Guidelines:
        1. Use Standardized Forms: Always rely on preprinted, approved forms drafted by attorneys or provided by your local Realtor association.
        1. Refer Legal Questions: If a client asks for legal counsel or document drafting outside real estate agency forms (such as drafting a power of attorney), politely decline and refer them to a qualified attorney.
  • Confidentiality & Compliance
    • Interconnected Fiduciary Duties: Both confidentiality and compliance are foundational brokerage duties rooted in respecting the client's wishes, tying directly back to the OLDCAR fiduciary duties of loyalty, obedience, and confidentiality.
    • The Flip-Side of Disclosure: Confidentiality is essentially the obligation of an agent not to disclose certain information, acting as the exact opposite of the duty of disclosure.
    • Guiding Principles of Confidentiality
      • Protecting Leverage: Never share information that weakens a client's negotiation position or leverage.
      • Key Examples of Confidential Information:
        • Motivations to buy or sell.
        • A buyer's ability or willingness to pay more than their offer.
        • A seller's willingness to accept less than the list price.
      • Client Autonomy vs. Legal Mandates: While clients generally dictate what personal information remains confidential, an agent's legal obligation to disclose material facts (such as property condition) is governed by state law, not client wishes.
    • The Duty of Compliance
      • Dual Definitions:
        1. Legal Adherence: The absolute obligation to carry out all brokerage and agency activities in strict accordance with the law.
        1. Synonym for Obedience: The duty to carry out the lawful instructions and wishes of the client, even if those wishes conflict with the agent's personal advice.
      • The Limit of Obedience (Compliance Trumps Obedience): While obedience requires following client directives, an agent must never obey an unlawful instruction. Compliance with the law always supersedes the duty of obedience.
  • The Duty of Disclosure
    • Core Significance: Disclosure is one of a brokerage's most critical responsibilities, carrying over from foundational agency principles.
    • Shared Responsibility: Because agency operates at the broker level, the principal broker's duty to disclose extends directly to all sponsored affiliate brokers acting on the broker's behalf.
    • The Golden Rule of Disclosures: Agents must always make full disclosures to their clients, erring on the side of disclosing everything rather than withholding information.
  • Disclosure of Agency & Property Condition
    • Timing: These two critical disclosures typically occur very early in the formation of an agency relationship and at the inception of a real estate transaction.
    • Purpose: They ensure complete transparency so that all parties understand who is represented and are fully informed about the physical and economic realities of the property before making decisions.
    • 1. Disclosure of Agency
      • Core Requirement: One of the most important disclosures a licensee makes.
      • What It Involves: Clients and customers must clearly understand:
        • Who is representing whom.
        • The precise scope and extent of that representation.
        • When any changes to the agency relationship occur.
    • 2. Property Condition & Material Facts
      • The Trigger: Transactions begin with a mutual interest in a property, placing an immediate duty on the seller (and their agent) to inform the buyer about the property's actual condition and related material facts.
      • Definition of Material Fact: Information that, if known, could alter a person's course of action or influence their purchasing decision.
      • Property Value Connection:
        • Property value is heavily tied to condition, alongside other factors like location and current market trends.
        • Licensees have a duty to help their respective clients estimate accurate property values—empowering sellers to set appropriate listing prices and buyers to make informed purchasing decisions.
  • Disclosure of All Offers
    • Timing: Offer disclosure typically falls in the middle stages of a transaction progression, right as negotiations heat up.
    • The Golden Rule of Offers: Agents must disclose all offers (good, bad, or indifferent) to their client promptly and as soon as reasonably possible.
    • Client Autonomy Trumps Personal Opinion: Even if an agent thinks an offer is terrible, or believes a better one is right around the corner, they cannot withhold it. The final decision to accept or reject belongs exclusively to the client, never the agent.
    • Real-World Pitfall Example
      • The Scenario: Janine (seller's agent) hides a very low offer from her seller client, Alberto, because she wants him to wait for a higher offer (which would yield a higher commission).
      • The Violation: Even if Janine's intuition is right and a higher offer arrives the next day, she has breached her duty by denying Alberto his legal right to review and decide on every offer submitted.
  • The Lowball Offer Scenario
    • The Setup: As a seller's agent for a uniquely eccentric, plant-themed mansion, you receive a sight-unseen offer that is only half of the asking price.
    • The Core Dilemma: Do you immediately present the lowball offer to your seller client, or do you withhold it because you feel insulted and want to wait for a better, higher-commission offer?
    • Option 1 (The Correct Choice - Full Disclosure):
      • Action: You promptly disclose the lowball offer to the seller despite your personal misgivings and desire for a higher commission.
      • Outcome: Although the offer is low, the seller reveals a hidden urgency (needing to sell quickly to fund a rare algae research trip to South Africa). Because you fulfilled your legal duty of disclosure, the seller retains control of their property, and you avoid catastrophic liability for withholding material negotiation details.
    • Option 2 (The Incorrect Choice - Withholding Information):
      • Action: You decide on your own authority to ignore and conceal the lowball offer.
      • Outcome: Weeks pass with zero other offers. Eventually, the buyer contacts the seller directly, exposing your concealment. The furious seller states they would have gladly accepted that offer to fund their South Africa move, leaving you facing severe legal liability, a breached fiduciary duty, and a ruined client relationship.
  • Disclosure: Financial Condition & Motivation
    • Negotiation Impact: A party's financial situation and underlying motivations directly dictate their bargaining power and leverage in a real estate transaction.
    • Fiduciary Responsibility: Agents must aggressively protect their own client's confidential financial details while strategically leveraging any insight they discover about the other party's financial standing or motivation to benefit their client.
    • Examples of How Financial Condition & Motivation Shape Negotiations:
      • Deep Pockets (Buyer): A buyer known to have abundant wealth will struggle to negotiate a lower purchase price because sellers won't budge.
      • Weak Financial Profile (Buyer): A buyer with poor financials appears far less competitive to a seller weighing multiple offers.
      • Relocation Urgency (Seller): A seller desperate to move quickly due to a new out-of-state job loses the leverage required to hold firm on a high asking price.
      • Financial Distress (Seller): A seller facing mounting debt or foreclosure sacrifice bargaining power during price negotiations.
    • The Essential Exception: Proof of Financing
      • The Rule vs. Reality: While a buyer's agent must guard a buyer's private financial details, they must disclose that the buyer possesses the financial ability or pre-approval to secure necessary financing.
      • Why: Without proof that a buyer is financially qualified, their purchase offer will appear unrealistic and completely uncompetitive to a seller.
  • Disclosure of Conflicts of Interest
    • Definition: A conflict of interest occurs when a licensee holds a personal stake or competing interest in a property or transaction.
    • The Rule: Whenever a conflict of interest arises, it must be fully disclosed immediately.
    • Common Categories of Conflicts:
      • Dual agency.
      • Confidentiality dilemmas.
      • Compensation issues.
      • Direct personal interest in the property or transaction.
    • Multiple Incomes & Compensation Disclosure
      • The Requirement: If a licensee will receive income or compensation from multiple sources tied to a transaction, that fact must be disclosed to all parties.
      • Why It Matters: Money complicates transactions. While receiving compensation does not automatically create an agency relationship, failing to disclose multiple income streams breeds suspicion, damages trust, and can lead to severe legal and ethical penalties if discovered later.
      • Transparency Standard: Even if a specific income source doesn't technically constitute a conflict of interest, erring on the side of full disclosure protects everyone involved and ensures absolute clarity regarding who is being paid and why.
  • Disclosure Timing
    • The Timeline: While general rules apply, the exact timing of disclosures varies based on the agency type and unique transaction dynamics. Furthermore, disclosures may need to be repeated multiple times throughout a single transaction.
    • The Golden Rule: Agency disclosure must occur during or before the event in which a customer becomes a client—specifically, always before signing an agency agreement.
    • Mandatory Disclosure Triggers
      1. When a customer becomes a client: The moment a casual interaction shifts into formal representation.
      1. Immediately upon a change in agency relationship: If an existing relationship shifts (such as moving from single agency to dual agency), disclosure must be immediate.
  • Informed Consent & First Substantive Contact
    • Informed Consent (Advance Informed Consent): The legal principle requiring that a party fully understands and agrees to proposed actions or agency relationships before they are implemented. If an agency relationship changes, it must be disclosed immediately.
    • First Substantive Contact: The critical trigger point requiring formal agency disclosure. It generally occurs at the first face-to-face meeting—or before any confidential information is shared or specific property action is taken.
  • What Constitutes First Substantive Contact?
    • A buyer expressing general interest in purchasing real estate.
    • A buyer meeting a seller's agent at an open house or property showing.
    • A seller sharing private financial details or their underlying motivation to sell.
    • Phone Rule: If first substantive contact happens over the phone, the agent must disclose verbally right away, followed by written confirmation as soon as possible.
    • Application Across Different Relationships
      • Listing Agent & Prospective Buyer/Tenant: Must disclose their existing agency relationship with the seller before or during first substantive contact (such as at a showing or before discussing confidential buyer details).
      • Listing Agent & Seller (Pre-Agreement): Must disclose that no relationship currently exists and explain what future agency representation would entail before signing the listing agreement.
      • Buyer's Agent & Buyer (Pre-Agreement): Must disclose the lack of a current relationship and explain what representation will entail before executing a buyer representation agreement.
      • Core Symmetry: The disclosure obligations for a buyer's agent meeting a prospective buyer mirror exactly those of a listing agent meeting a prospective seller.

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Brokerage Duties: Fulfilling the Agent-Client Contract
    • Agency relationship contracts should spell out the duties of the agent and client. Preprinted contracts available through your broker and/or local Realtor association will likely serve as the framework for your agreements. You will fill in the blanks and add attachments to those contracts to customize them to your client's needs.
    • If something more unique is needed, you might consider having an attorney draft the contract.
  • Brokerage Duties:
    • Seeking Out the Other Party - As you will typically represent a principal on one side of a transaction, your first objective will be to help find the other party so that a deal can be negotiated. And throughout the process, you'll be constantly engaged in the effort of helping your client put their best foot forward as they interact with the other party in the negotiations.
    • Appropriate Advice - Giving appropriate advice is a brokerage duty that requires a level of expertise and competency of the license holder that the client will not have. But even as the agent gives their expert advice, they need to take care not to venture into the unauthorized practice of law.
    • Confidentiality - Confidentiality is an OLDCAR fiduciary duty owed to clients. The principle behind it is that the agent should take care to never disclose anything that would lessen the client's leverage in a negotiation. This duty is tempered by the agent's obligation to disclose material facts, particularly as they pertain to property condition, which disclosures are mandated by state law.
    • Compliance - The duty of compliance requires the agent to carry out all brokerage duties according to the law. Even the fiduciary duty of obedience to a client gives way to the duty of compliance to the law.
  • Two Primary Categories of Disclosure
    • Disclosure of agency
    • Disclosure of material facts
