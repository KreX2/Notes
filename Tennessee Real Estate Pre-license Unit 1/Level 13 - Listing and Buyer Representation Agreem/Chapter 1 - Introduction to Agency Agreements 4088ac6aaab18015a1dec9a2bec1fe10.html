Chapter 1 - Introduction to Agency Agreements

Agency Agreements: The Basics

  • Definition: An agency agreement is a mutually binding contract that authorizes an agent to act on a client's behalf to achieve a specific real estate goal. They are frequently referred to as employment agreements.
  • The Written Requirement in Tennessee: In Tennessee, a written, signed agency agreement is legally required to establish an agency relationship. Handing over an agency disclosure form is not enough.
  • No Accidental Agency: Because a written agreement is mandatory, you cannot accidentally fall into an agency relationship—it must be created intentionally.
  • Primary Types of Agency Agreements
    • Listing Agreement: The contract between a seller and a broker.
    • Buyer Representation Agreement: The contract between a buyer and a broker.
    • Transaction Coordinator Agreement: Serves a distinctly different administrative purpose, which we will explore later.
  • Nature of the Client-Broker Relationship
    • Task-Oriented: Unlike traditional ongoing employer-employee relationships, real estate agency agreements are strictly focused on completing a specific service or objective (like selling a home, buying a property, or leasing commercial space) and terminate once that objective is met.
  • The Details
    • Types of Agency in Agreements:
      • Traditional Agency: Established between the client and the managing broker (referred to as the principal broker in Tennessee). This automatically creates an agency relationship with all affiliate brokers working under that principal broker, even if only one affiliate does the actual legwork.
      • Designated Agency: Established between a specific affiliate broker and a client, isolating the relationship so it does not extend to other licensees in the brokerage.
    • Core Legal Rules for Tennessee Agency Agreements
      • Must Be in Writing & Bilateral: Agency cannot exist without a written, signed agreement executed by both parties. Until this contract is signed, the licensee defaults to being a facilitator (not a representative).
      • No Interfering with Exclusive Agreements: Tennessee license law strictly prohibits a licensee from procuring or attempting to procure an employment agreement from a party already bound by an existing exclusive agreement (e.g., you cannot poach Client Callie from Broker Brenda).
      • State Does Not Provide Forms: The State of Tennessee does not promulgate or provide standardized agency contract forms. Licensees typically rely on forms provided by Tennessee REALTORS® or drafted with the help of an attorney, in addition to their supervising broker's approved templates.
      • Non-Assignable: Agency agreements are personal service contracts and cannot be assigned to another broker without the explicit, written consent of all parties involved at the time of the assignment.
  • Agency Disclosure: A Review
    • The Prerequisite Rule: Before signing any employment agreement, a licensee must deliver the agency disclosure form in writing. An agency relationship cannot legally be established without it.
    • First Substantive Contact (The Trigger): The critical turning point requiring formal agency disclosure. It occurs at the first face-to-face meeting (or equivalent substantive interaction) before any confidential information is shared or specific property discussions/actions take place.
    • Informed Consent (The Principle): Parties must fully understand and consent to agency terms and existing representations prior to implementation. Even if a customer chooses not to enter into representation, they still have a right to know about any existing agency relationships the licensee holds.
  • Agency Employment Agreements
    • Prerequisite Reminder: Before executing either type of employment agreement, the required written agency disclosure must be delivered to ensure informed consent.
    1. Listing Agreements
      • Parties: Between a property owner/seller (principal) and a broker.
      • Function: Authorizes the broker and their affiliated licensees to market and assist in the sale of real property.
      • Status Change: Automatically transforms a customer into a client and the broker into the listing agent.
    1. Buyer Representation Agreements
      • Also Known As: Buyer-agency agreement or buyer-broker agreement.
      • Parties: Between a prospective buyer (principal) and a broker.
      • Function: Authorizes the broker and their affiliates to assist the buyer in locating, negotiating, and procuring real property.
      • Status Change: Transforms a customer into a client and the broker into the buyer's agent.
  • Transaction Coordinator Agreement
    • Newer Industry Role: Transaction coordinator agreements are a relatively modern addition to real estate practice and serve a fundamentally different purpose than traditional agency agreements.
    • Neutral Middleman (Zero Agency): Instead of representing the buyer or the seller, the transaction coordinator/facilitator represents neither party. Their primary role is purely administrative: managing, organizing, and facilitating the paperwork and closing documents of the transaction.
    • Strict Boundaries (No Advice or Negotiation): Because they operate without an agency relationship, facilitators owe no fiduciary duties to anyone. They are strictly prohibited from giving advice, counseling, or negotiating on behalf of either party. Crossing that line violates their non-agency status.
    • Industry Perception: Due to the extreme difficulty of remaining truly neutral while serving both sides of a transaction, transaction brokerage is viewed with caution and is not recommended by many real estate professionals.
  • Agency Law and Contract Law
    • The Dual Legal Domain: Agency agreements sit at the intersection of two distinct legal frameworks: agency law and contract law. Because they are literally written contracts governing agency practice, they must comply with both.
    • 1. Agency Law
      • What it Governs: Regulates the structural practice of agency, including the specific roles of brokers and clients, the fiduciary duties owed by the agent, and the precise scope of the agent's authority.
      • The Tennessee Override Rule: In Tennessee, statutory real estate agency law completely supersedes and overrules the common law of agency. If statutory law and common law conflict, Tennessee statutory law always wins.
    • 2. Contract Law
      • What it Governs: Regulates the formation and execution of contracts, covering essential legal pillars such as contract validity requirements, proper legal form, and the lawful methods for terminating a contract.
  • Terminating an Agency Agreement
  • Termination by Performance
    • The Gold Standard: The absolute most desirable way to terminate a real estate employment agreement is through the complete fulfillment of its purpose (e.g., the house successfully sells or the buyer closes on a property).
    • Definition: Performance is the legal fulfillment of an obligation, duty, or contract.
    • What Performance Looks Like in Action
      • A listing agent successfully bringing a ready, willing, and able buyer who purchases the property.
      • A buyer's agent helping evaluate properties, market conditions, or structure winning offers.
      • A listing agent executing marketing campaigns and negotiating favorable purchase prices.
    • Performance and Compensation
      • Tied Together: Compensation is directly linked to performance, and every employment agreement must clearly outline the broker's compensation terms.
      • The Broker Flow Rule: All compensation must be paid directly to the employing/principal broker, who then distributes the appropriate split to the sponsored affiliate brokers who handled the transaction.
    • Performance in a Listing Agreement
      • For a seller's listing agreement, performance is typically achieved when the agent produces a ready, willing, and able buyer; someone who is fully prepared, legally qualified, and financially able to close the transaction on the seller's terms.
  • Due Diligence
    • Definition: Due diligence refers to the reasonable steps, investigations, and verifications a person must take to fulfill a legal requirement or standard of care. It is the direct opposite of negligence.
    • Role of the Agent: Licensees exercise due diligence by actively verifying critical transaction details rather than blindly relying on unverified assumptions.
    • What Due Diligence Looks Like in Practice
      • Verifying the accuracy of property listing details and marketing claims.
      • Checking the physical condition of a property or reviewing inspection disclosures.
      • Investigating the legal ownership status of a property (title checks).
      • Confirming the legal authority or eligibility of other parties to act in the transaction.

Key Terms

  • buyer representation agreement - a written agreement by which a real estate broker is entitled to compensation for services rendered to assist the buyer in the procurement of real property
  • due diligence - a legal term used to describe the reasonable steps a person must take in order to satisfy a legal requirement
  • performance - the fulfillment of an obligation, duty, or contract

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Real Estate Employment Agreements
    • In Tennessee, agency agreements relate to the following types of agency:
    • Traditional agency agreements are between the client and the managing broker, which in effect creates an agency relationship between the client and all of the affiliate brokers working for that broker. Even though one of the broker's licensed affiliates may be doing all the legwork, they are acting on behalf of the managing broker. (Remember that the managing broker is called principal broker in Tennessee.)
    • Designated agency agreements are between a specific affiliate broker and a client. This means it does not establish relationships between the client and other licensees in the brokerage.
  • In addition, agency agreements:
    • Must be written, bilateral agreements
    • Are not assignable
    • Are not promulgated, meaning the state of Tennessee does not provide standardized forms
  • General Types of Employment Agreements
    • Written agency between a broker and a seller will usually take the form of a listing agreement, in which a principal authorizes the broker and their affiliate brokers to assist the principal in the sale of real property. Signing this agreement transforms the customer into a client and the broker into a listing agent.
    • Written agency between a broker and a buyer will usually take the form of a buyer representation agreement in which a buyer authorizes the broker and their affiliate brokers to assist the buyer in the procurement of real property. Signing this agreement transforms a buyer into a client and the broker into a buyer's agent.
    • In a transaction coordinator agreement, instead of representing either of the parties in a sale, a transaction broker represents neither party. They simply facilitate the paperwork of the transaction and do not owe fiduciary duties to either party.
  • Terminating Employment Agreements
    • Here are the ways a written employment agreement can come to an end:
      • Expiration
      • Infeasibility
      • Mutual agreement
      • Abandonment
      • Breach or invalidity
      • Revocation
      • Performance (most desirable!)
