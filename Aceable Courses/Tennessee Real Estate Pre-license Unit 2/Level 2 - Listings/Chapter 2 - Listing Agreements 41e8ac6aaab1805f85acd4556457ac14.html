Chapter 2 - Listing Agreements

Definition & Purpose of a Listing Agreement

  • Employment Contract Between Seller/Landlord and Broker: A listing agreement is a written, bilateral employment contract (T.C.A. § 62-13-401) in which a property owner (principal) hires a licensed brokerage firm to market their property and procure a buyer or tenant. It is not a purchase-and-sale contract or a lease agreement, even after a buyer or tenant is found.
  • Authorizes Listing Agency & Fiduciary Duties: Executing a listing agreement establishes an agency relationship authorizing the licensee to represent the principal and market the property to customers, imposing full statutory and fiduciary client duties (T.C.A. § 62-13-404 / OLDCAR) on the brokerage.
  • Scope of Brokerage Obligations: The broker agrees to provide all contracted real estate marketing and transaction services until the property is sold or leased, and may utilize sponsored affiliate brokers and outside cooperating brokers to carry out these duties under the principal broker's supervision.
  • Non-Promulgated Forms in Tennessee: The State of Tennessee (TREC) does not promulgate a mandatory standardized listing form. Brokers may use an attorney-drafted company form or preprinted forms supplied by Tennessee REALTORS® (such as Form RF101 for Exclusive Right to Sell — Designated Agency or Form RF102 for Exclusive Right to Sell — Seller Agency).

  • Traditional Agency vs. Designated Agency in Tennessee Listing Agreements (T.C.A. § 62-13-406)
    • Traditional (Firm-Wide) Agency: The listing agreement is between the seller and the managing/principal broker, automatically making every affiliate broker sponsored by that firm an agent of the seller. If another licensee in the same office brings a buyer client to that listing, an in-house dual agency conflict is created.
    • Designated Agency (T.C.A. § 62-13-406): The listing agreement appoints one specific affiliate broker as the seller's Designated Agent, to the exclusion of all other licensees in the brokerage. Confidential client information is walled off from other agents and the managing broker (T.C.A. § 62-13-406(c)), preventing imputed knowledge and allowing another licensee in the same firm to represent a buyer as their own designated agent without creating dual agency.
  • Open Listings (Non-Exclusive & Unilateral)
    • Definition & Contract Nature: An open listing is a non-exclusive, unilateral contract in which a property owner grants multiple brokers—as well as the owner themselves—the concurrent right to market and sell the property. The broker is not legally obligated to market or perform, but if a broker produces a ready, willing, and able buyer, the seller is obligated to pay that broker's commission.
    • Tennessee Written Requirement: Even though open listings operate as unilateral employment offers, Tennessee law (T.C.A. § 62-13-401) requires any agency relationship to be documented in a written agreement.
    • Pros & Cons for Sellers:
      • Advantages: Multiple brokerages can expose the property to more buyers; the seller owes zero commission if the seller finds their own buyer; and the seller may revoke or terminate any open listing at any time prior to performance.
      • Disadvantages: Rare in residential real estate because brokers have minimal financial certainty and are reluctant to invest marketing funds or share specialized expertise when competing against other brokers and the owner.
    • Procuring Cause: Because multiple brokers may hold open listings simultaneously, a broker must prove they were the procuring cause—the immediate, uninterrupted, and efficient series of actions that led the buyer to purchase the property—in order to claim the commission.
  • Exclusive Right-to-Sell Listings
    • Maximum Broker Protection: The most common and preferred residential listing agreement, granting one named listing brokerage the exclusive right to market and sell the property for a specified contract term.
    • Commission Earned Regardless of Who Finds the Buyer: If the property sells during the listing period, the named listing broker is entitled to the agreed commission no matter who procured the buyer—whether the listing broker, a cooperating broker, or the seller acting independently without agent assistance.
    • Cooperating Broker Compensation: When another licensee (such as a buyer's agent) brings the buyer, compensation is shared or paid according to the written listing terms and any cooperative compensation offer authorized by the seller. (Note: Under Tennessee REALTORS® forms such as RF101 for Designated Agency and RF102 for Seller Agency, the seller specifies in writing whether cooperative compensation is offered.)
  • Exclusive Agency Listings
    • Hybrid Structure: Combines the single-broker exclusivity of an exclusive right-to-sell listing with the seller's self-sale exception from an open listing:
      • One Broker Only: The seller agrees not to list the property with any other brokerage during the listing term.
      • Seller Self-Sale Exception: The listing broker earns a commission if the property is sold by anyone other than the owner (including the listing broker or any cooperating broker), unless the owner independently procures their own buyer.
    • Practical Caveats & Named Exclusions: Favored by sellers who want professional representation while retaining the option to sell to an acquaintance or prospect they find themselves. To prevent procuring cause disputes, sellers frequently name specific known prospective buyers as written exclusions at the start of the agreement. (Note: In Tennessee, net listings—where a broker keeps everything above a seller's minimum net price—are strictly prohibited under TREC Rule 1260-02-.01.)

  • Commission Eligibility
    • Note that the seller is the one paying the commission. So when we say that the seller is eligible for a commission, they're not literally paying themselves, but rather, they may not have to pay a commission to anyone in those circumstances.
  • Net Listings: Definition, Prohibition & Practical Risks
    • How a Net Listing Works: In a net listing, the seller specifies a minimum net dollar amount they will accept from the sale of their property, and any sale proceeds exceeding that threshold are retained by the broker as compensation.
    • Strictly Illegal in Tennessee (TREC Rule 1260-02-.07): Net price listings are illegal in Tennessee and many other states. Under TREC Rule 1260-02-.07, "No broker or affiliate broker shall accept or enter a listing based on a 'net price' (i.e., a price excluding the customary commission and expenses associated with the sale)."
    • Why Net Listings Are Prohibited & Risky:
      • Severe Conflict of Interest & Fraud Risk: A dishonest licensee could exploit an uninformed seller's lack of market knowledge to set an artificially low net price and pocket a massive, windfall profit.
      • Financial Risk to Ethical Licensees: Because a licensee's fiduciary duty requires presenting all offers (T.C.A. § 62-13-404(3)), if a seller accepts a bare-minimum offer equal to their net target, the licensee earns little or zero commission despite full marketing work.
  • Pre-Listing Preparation & Gathering Property Information
    • Upfront Information Gathering: Before completing a listing agreement, the affiliate broker should collect comprehensive property details from the seller—including dimensions, taxes, utilities, HOA fees, updates, defects, and disclosures—to ensure accurate MLS input and marketing.
    • Building Trust & Efficiency: Mastering the property's details upfront enables the licensee to answer buyer and cooperating agent questions immediately without repeatedly interrupting the seller, building confidence with both sides of the transaction.
  • Preview: Tennessee REALTORS® Form RF101 (Exclusive Right to Sell Listing Agreement — Designated Agency)
    • Most Common Tennessee Residential Listing Form: Because many Tennessee brokerages default to designated agency (T.C.A. § 62-13-406), Form RF101 is the standard industry contract used by Tennessee REALTORS® members to establish an exclusive right-to-sell listing under designated agency (contrasted with Form RF102, which establishes firm-wide traditional seller agency).
    • Core Structure Covered in the Walkthrough:
      • Grant of Exclusive Right to Sell & Property Identification: Appoints the brokerage exclusively to sell the specifically described property for a definite start and end date (T.C.A. § 62-13-312(b)(9) requires a definite termination date on every listing agreement).
      • Compensation & Cooperative Compensation Authorization: Establishes the total brokerage fee, carry-over protection period, and the seller's written instructions regarding whether cooperative compensation is offered to cooperating brokers/facilitators.
      • Responsibilities, Marketing & Disclosures: Authorizes MLS entry, internet advertising, signage, lockbox access, and property showings, while outlining the seller's duty to complete the Tennessee Residential Property Condition Disclosure and convey marketable title via warranty deed.
      • Designated Agency Appointment & Facilitator Fallback: Names the specific affiliate broker appointed as the seller's Designated Agent (T.C.A. § 62-13-406) and sets the protocol if that same licensee also represents a prospective buyer (defaulting to neutral Facilitator status unless otherwise agreed in writing).
  • Part 1: Parties, Property Identification, Listing Price, Term & Carry-Over Clause
    • Contracting Parties: Even when an affiliate broker is appointed as the seller's Designated Agent, the listing agreement is legally between the principal/managing broker (firm) and the seller (principal).
    • Essential Core Elements: Every Exclusive Right-to-Sell Listing Agreement (Form RF101) specifies:
      • Legal description and street address of the property
      • Initial listing price (the seller's asking price—not a guarantee of final sale price)
      • Definite expiration date (mandated by T.C.A. § 62-13-312(b)(9) for every Tennessee listing agreement)
      • Brokerage compensation structure and payment terms
      • Respective obligations of the broker and the property owner
    • Carry-Over (Protection) Clause: Protects the brokerage's earned commission for a negotiated post-expiration period if the property sells to a buyer introduced during the listing term (unless relisted under an exclusive agreement with another licensed broker).
  • Part 2: Compensation, Leasing Fallback & Fair Housing Compliance
    • Negotiability of Compensation: Both the listing brokerage's commission and any cooperative compensation offered to a buyer's agent or facilitator are always 100% negotiable and never fixed by law or REALTOR® associations.
    • Leasing Fallback Provision: If the seller decides to lease or rent the property instead of selling it during the listing period (or carry-over period), the seller still owes the brokerage the agreed lease compensation specified in the contract.
    • Fair Housing & Non-Discrimination Mandate: Strictly prohibits discrimination against any prospective buyer or tenant based on race, creed, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, or national origin under federal Fair Housing laws and the Tennessee Human Rights Act (T.C.A. § 4-21-601).
  • Part 3: Disclosures, Scope of Expertise & Facilitator Status Fallback
    • Seller Property Disclosure Responsibility: The licensee must ensure the seller completes the state-mandated Tennessee Residential Property Condition Disclosure form (T.C.A. § 66-5-201) and understands their legal duty to disclose all known material facts.
    • Scope of Expertise & Expert Referrals: Licensees are real estate professionals—not engineers, lawyers, or tax advisors—and must advise clients in writing to retain qualified outside specialists for:
      • Property and structural inspections
      • Environmental hazards (radon, lead-based paint, mold, asbestos)
      • Public controls (zoning, building codes, permits)
      • Tax and legal matters
    • Temporary Shift to Facilitator Status: If the seller's appointed Designated Agent also represents a prospective buyer who wants to make an offer on the listing, the licensee cannot serve as designated agent for both sides and is authorized to temporarily change status to a neutral Facilitator (with written confirmation) unless another arrangement is agreed upon.
  • Part 4: Home Protection Plans, Contract Modifications & Special Stipulations
    • Home Protection Plan (Home Warranty): The contract includes an option for the seller to offer or purchase a home protection plan (warranty covering mechanical systems and appliances for a set period post-closing) as a marketing incentive.
    • Written Modification Only: Once signed, the listing agreement cannot be altered, amended, or terminated without the written consent of all parties.
    • Severability Clause: If any single provision of the contract is held invalid or unenforceable by a court, that provision is severed while all remaining terms remain valid and enforceable.
    • Special Stipulations / Considerations: Custom language added to the Special Stipulations section overrides conflicting pre-printed contract language—so licensees should use it rarely and with caution, ensuring any custom terms are clear, lawful, and do not constitute the unauthorized practice of law.

  • Signatures & Contact Information on Listing Agreements
    • All Co-Owners Must Sign: Whenever a property is owned by more than one person (such as spouses, co-owners, or joint tenants), every legal owner must sign and date the listing agreement for it to be fully valid and binding. If someone is signing on behalf of an owner (such as under a Power of Attorney, trust, or corporate entity), obtain a notarized copy of the authorizing document for the brokerage file.
    • Complete Seller Contact Details: Record each owner's printed/typed legal name, mailing address for written notices, telephone number, and email address accurately—under Tennessee REALTORS® Form RF101, the seller also agrees to keep the broker informed of their whereabouts so notices and offers can be delivered promptly.
    • Brokerage Signatures & Copy Delivery (T.C.A. § 62-13-312(b)(8)): Both the authorized brokerage representative (principal broker or authorized designated agent) and the seller(s) must sign and date the agreement, and the licensee must promptly provide a true, signed copy of the executed listing agreement to every signatory.

Key Concepts & Principles

  • A Review of Listing Types
    • Open listing: a nonexclusive listing agreement that gives multiple brokers (and owners themselves) the right to sell the property
    • Exclusive right-to-sell listing: an agreement in which the seller guarantees the named broker receives a commission if the property is sold, regardless of who brings the buyer
    • Exclusive agency listing: an agreement in which the seller has an exclusive relationship with a broker but retains the right to sell the property to named prospects
    • Net listing: an agreement in which the seller names an amount they will accept for a property and if the sales price exceeds that amount, the broker receives the excess as commission; illegal in Tennessee

    • Some important parts of the agreement:
      • The listing price is the initial asking price; it is not guaranteed that the property will sell for that price.
      • Listing agreements must have an expiration date.
      • The carry-over clause protects agents' commission; it specifies a limited time frame after the agreement ends during which an agent can be owed compensation for finding or attracting a buyer.
      • An agent's compensation is always negotiated between the seller and the licensee.
      • Discrimination against a buyer because of their race, creed, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin is against the law.
      • Compensation for cooperating agents, such as the buyer's agent or facilitator, is always negotiable.
      • Clients will likely need to seek expert advice when it comes to inspections, material facts related to environmental hazards, material facts related to public controls, taxes, and law.
