Chapter 5 - Buyer Representation Agreements

Principles of Agency Creation in Tennessee

  • Written Bilateral Agreement Requirement (T.C.A. § 62-13-401): While general common law in many states allows agency to be created orally or by implication/action, Tennessee statutory law strictly prohibits implied or accidental agency. In Tennessee real estate, an agency relationship is created only through a written bilateral agreement—an exchange of mutual promises between the principal (client) and the principal broker (firm).
  • Two Essential Components of Agency:
    1. Consent: Mutual agreement where both the principal and the agent consent to the representation relationship.
    1. Control: Legal authority granted to the agent to act on the principal's behalf within the boundaries defined by their written agreement.
  • Express Agency in Tennessee: Express agency occurs when a principal explicitly appoints an agent to act for them. In Tennessee real estate practice, express agency is established exclusively through two written instruments: a listing agreement (for sellers/landlords) or a buyer/tenant representation agreement (for buyers/tenants). (Disclosure forms such as RF301 or RF302 alone never create an agency relationship.)
  • Buyer Representation Agreements: Core Structure & Elements
    • Nature of the Contract: A buyer representation agreement is a written, bilateral employment contract between a buyer (principal) and a sponsoring principal broker (firm) authorizing the brokerage to represent the buyer in locating and acquiring property. Once executed, the brokerage owes full statutory and fiduciary client duties (T.C.A. § 62-13-404) to the buyer regardless of whether the seller, buyer, or both ultimately fund the broker's commission.
    • Non-Promulgated Forms: The State of Tennessee (TREC) does not promulgate standard contract forms, though most Tennessee licensees use preprinted forms provided by Tennessee REALTORS® (such as Form RF141 for designated agency or RF142 for traditional buyer agency). Under TREC Rule 1260-02-.36, exclusive buyer representation agreements must also explicitly confirm in writing how the buyer arranges property viewings and whether the buyer would owe a commission if they purchase a property without the broker.

  • Exclusivity vs. Non-Exclusivity in Buyer Agreements
    • One-Way Exclusivity: Buyer representation agreements may be either exclusive or non-exclusive, and exclusivity operates as a one-way commitment from the buyer to the brokerage:
      • Buyer's Exclusive Commitment: Under an exclusive buyer representation agreement, the buyer grants that single brokerage the exclusive right to locate a property and warrants they have no active agreement with another broker. If the buyer purchases a property during the agreement term—even through another licensee or directly from an owner—the buyer still owes the agreed compensation to the exclusive broker (as required to be explicitly disclosed under TREC Rule 1260-02-.36).
      • Other Buyers Acknowledgment: The broker is not restricted to working with only one buyer; the contract includes an acknowledgment that the broker represents other buyers who may view, bid on, and compete for the same properties.
    • Core Broker Duties Under the Agreement:
      • Assist the buyer in locating a suitable property (often within a defined geographic market area).
      • Negotiate the purchase price and contract terms on the buyer's behalf.
      • Guide and assist the buyer through transaction milestones, including earnest money deposit handling, inspections, and closing.
  • Buyer's Agent Compensation Structures
    • Following the NAR settlement practice changes (effective August 2024 and affirmed on appeal in August 2026), offers of cooperative compensation can no longer be published on the MLS. While a buyer can still request in their offer that the seller pay their agent's fee (or accept an off-MLS seller offer), the buyer is directly responsible for paying their agent if the seller does not pay—and the agent cannot collect more from any source than the objectively ascertainable amount capped in the written buyer agreement.

  • Written Buyer-Agency Agreement Triggers & Exceptions
    • When a Signed Written Agreement Is Required ("Working With" a Buyer): Under NAR MLS rules, an MLS-participating licensee must execute a written buyer representation agreement (specifying services and an objectively ascertainable fee cap) before touring a property with a buyer—including both in-person showings and live virtual tours. "Working with" a buyer includes identifying tailored listings, arranging or conducting tours, negotiating terms, or preparing/presenting an offer.
    • When a Written Buyer Agreement Is NOT Required:
      • Chatting with an unrepresented visitor at an open house or answering general questions about real estate services.
      • Listing Agent Showing Their Own Listing: When a listing agent tours their own seller-client's property with an unrepresented buyer (such as at an open house or private inquiry), the listing agent is working for the seller, not "working with" the buyer—provided the listing agent properly discloses their seller-agency status.
    • Dual Agency Exception: If a listing agent (or firm) also represents the buyer in an authorized disclosed dual agency transaction, the licensee is working with the buyer and must execute a signed written buyer representation agreement along with written dual-agency disclosure and informed consent from both parties.
  • Two Key Dimensions of Buyer Representation Agreements
    • Buyer representation agreements differ across two primary structural choices:
      1. Exclusivity (Exclusive vs. Non-Exclusive): Determines whether the buyer is bound to purchase exclusively through one brokerage/agent during the contract term (guaranteeing compensation if the buyer buys a covered property) or retains the right to work with multiple brokers and find properties independently (where compensation is earned only if that specific broker/agent procures the property).
      1. Agency Structure (Designated Agency vs. Traditional Buyer Agency): Because many Tennessee brokerages default to designated agency (T.C.A. § 62-13-406), representation can be assigned either to one specific affiliate broker appointed by the managing broker (to the exclusion of other licensees in the firm) or to the entire brokerage firm as a whole.

  • Practical & Exam Takeaways on Tennessee Buyer Rep Forms
    • Odd vs. Even Numbering Pattern: Forms ending in an odd number (RF141, RF143) establish Designated Agency with a single named affiliate broker, whereas forms ending in an even number (RF142, RF144) establish firm-wide Buyer Agency.
    • Same-Agent In-House Fallback on RF141/RF143: Under Tennessee designated agency agreements, if the appointed Designated Agent for the buyer also represents the seller of a property the buyer wants to buy, the licensee cannot act as a designated agent for both sides and must immediately notify both parties and default to Facilitator status (confirmed in writing prior to contract execution) unless another arrangement is agreed upon.
    • Carry-Over (Protection) Clause: Exclusive buyer representation agreements (RF141 and RF142) typically include a post-expiration carry-over period protecting the broker's fee if the buyer purchases a property introduced by the broker during the agreement term—unless the buyer has subsequently signed a valid buyer representation agreement with another licensed broker.
  • Overview & Contract Nature (Form RF141)
    • Contract Classification: Buyer representation agreements in Tennessee are written, bilateral employment contracts (T.C.A. § 62-13-401) establishing mutual promises between the buyer and the brokerage.
    • Non-Promulgated Status: Tennessee does not promulgate state-mandated contract forms; Form RF141 (Exclusive Buyer Representation Agreement — Designated Agency) is provided by Tennessee REALTORS® exclusively for its members.
    • Designated Agency Default: Many Tennessee brokerages operate by default under designated agency (T.C.A. § 62-13-406), where the managing/principal broker appoints one specific affiliate broker to represent the buyer exclusively, isolating confidential information from the rest of the firm.
    • Essential Contract Requirements: Every buyer representation agreement must specify:
      • Defined start and stop dates
      • Compensation structure and payment terms
      • Mutual promises/obligations of the parties
      • The agent's exact degree of authority
  • Sections 1–2: Parties, Purpose, Term & Desired Property
    • Section 1 (Parties, Purpose & Term): Identifies the buyer(s), the sponsoring brokerage/firm, the employment purpose, and the exact commencement and expiration dates of the agreement.
    • Section 2 (Property Description): Defines the general characteristics and criteria of the property the buyer seeks to acquire (property type, general location, size, or price range) rather than a single street address.
  • Sections 3–4: Exclusivity, Compensation, Carry-Over Clause & Agency Duties
    • Buyer's Exclusivity Commitments (Section 3):
      • Agrees not to enter into a buyer representation agreement with any other broker during the contract term.
      • Agrees to cooperate with the appointed designated agent and work through them when viewing or inquiring about properties.
      • Acknowledges that the brokerage earns its agreed compensation if the buyer purchases a qualifying property during the term, regardless of how the buyer located the property (including FSBOs or properties found online).
      • Establishes when and how the compensation is earned and payable (typically at closing or upon buyer default on an accepted purchase contract).
    • Compensation Rules & Payer of Last Resort:
      • Commission/fee rates are 100% negotiable and never fixed by law.
      • Compensation is commonly funded from seller proceeds (via an off-MLS cooperative offer or negotiated into the purchase offer), but the buyer is the payer of last resort responsible for any shortfall if the seller pays nothing or pays less than the agreed fee.
    • Carry-Over (Protection) Clause:
      • Protects the agent/brokerage for a negotiable post-expiration period if the buyer purchases a property introduced or shown by the agent during the agreement term (unless the buyer subsequently signs a valid representation agreement with another licensed broker).
    • Agency Definitions & Statutory Duties (Section 4):
      • Defines key representation terms (T.C.A. § 62-13-402).
      • Lists the baseline statutory duties owed to all parties (T.C.A. § 62-13-403: reasonable skill/care, disclosure of known adverse facts, honesty/good faith, pre-agency confidentiality, accounting, and no undisclosed self-dealing).
      • Lists the additional fiduciary/statutory duties owed exclusively to clients (T.C.A. § 62-13-404: obedience to lawful instructions, loyalty, scheduling showings, promptly presenting offers/counteroffers, negotiating assistance, and guiding post-contract closing steps).
  • Sections 4–8: Buyer Obligations, Authorizations, Confidentiality, Earnest Money & Limitations
    • Buyer Communications & Direct Contact Rules:
      • The buyer must not contact listing agents directly and must immediately inform any seller's agent (such as at an open house) that they are already represented.
      • Reaffirms that the buyer's brokerage is owed compensation for any covered property purchase during the term.
    • Buyer's Authorizations:
      • Appoints the named affiliate broker as the buyer's Designated Agent (T.C.A. § 62-13-406).
      • Authorizes the managing/principal broker to assign a subsequent designated agent if the original agent becomes unavailable.
      • Authorizes an automatic default to neutral Facilitator (transaction broker) status if the buyer's designated agent also represents the seller of a property the buyer wishes to buy—with the option to resume designated agency if the buyer decides not to pursue that property.
    • Confidentiality: Establishes strict confidentiality of the buyer's personal, financial, and motivational information unless the buyer gives explicit written authorization to share specific details (such as pre-approval strength during negotiations).
    • Earnest Money / Trust Money: Authorizes the brokerage to accept, deposit, and hold earnest money/trust funds in an escrow/trust account to be applied toward the purchase price at closing (T.C.A. § 62-13-321).
    • Limitations of Representation:
      • Confirms that the brokerage and the designated agent may show the same properties to other prospective buyers without breaching loyalty.
      • Clarifies that the licensee is not expected to possess expertise outside real estate brokerage (such as engineering, surveying, law, tax, or environmental testing).
    • Expert Assistance: Counsels the buyer to hire qualified outside professionals (home inspectors, surveyors, attorneys, lenders, contractors) and clarifies that providing a list of vendor referrals does not constitute a warranty or guarantee of their work.
