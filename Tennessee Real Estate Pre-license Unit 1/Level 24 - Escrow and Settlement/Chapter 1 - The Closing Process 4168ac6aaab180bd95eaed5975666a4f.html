Chapter 1 - The Closing Process

Closing (Settlement)

  • Definition: The final consummation of a real estate transaction where title to the property is officially transferred from the seller to the buyer in exchange for consideration (most commonly money), and the buyer's mortgage loan funds are disbursed.
  • Contract Execution Status: Once closing is completed, the real estate purchase contract has been fully executed (all promises and obligations have been fulfilled).
  • Where Closings Are Held: Specified in the purchase contract and may take place at:
    • The title company's office
    • The buyer's lending institution
    • A closing attorney's office
    • A real estate broker's office
  • Roles of Parties at Closing
    • While not every party attends every closing in person, each participant has a distinct legal or financial role:
      PartyRole & Responsibilities During Closing
      BuyerPays the purchase consideration (down payment and loan funds) and receives legal title to the property.
      SellerReceives the net sale proceeds and executes/delivers the deed transferring title to the buyer.
      Real Estate AgentAccounts for all transacted monies (such as earnest money deposits and commission disbursements), fulfilling their fiduciary duty of accounting.
      Buyer's & Seller's AttorneysReview closing documents to protect their respective client's legal interests and conduct the closing. (In Tennessee, hiring an attorney is optional, so either party may choose whether to retain one.)
      Lender Representatives / AttorneysReview loan and title documents, prepare settlement statements, and disburse mortgage funds.
      Title Company RepresentativesReview closing documents, verify clear title, record instruments, and deliver evidence that the title is insured (title insurance policy).

  • Closing Agent (Settlement Agent)
    • Definition: The neutral professional or representative who coordinates, oversees, and conducts the real estate closing.
    • Core Duties of the Closing Agent:
      1. Assemble all documents needed to finalize the buyer's loan and the seller's transfer of title.
      1. Prepare the Closing Disclosure in accordance with the purchase contract and the lender's instructions.
      1. Arrange the time and location of closing with all parties.
      1. Conduct the closing (in most transactions).
  • The Closing Disclosure (RESPA / TRID Rules)
    • Statutory Background: Enacted in 1974, the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA) regulates the settlement and lending process for federally related mortgage loans.
    • Replacement of HUD-1 (2015): In 2015, the older HUD-1 Settlement Statement was replaced by the Closing Disclosure (also referred to as the closing statement or settlement statement), which consolidates the former HUD-1, Good Faith Estimate (GFE), and Truth in Lending Act (TILA) disclosures into one standardized form.
    • Purpose & Timing Rules:
      • Provides the final, exact itemization of all loan terms, settlement services, and fees charged to the borrower.
      • 3-Business-Day Delivery Rule: For loans requiring a Loan Estimate, the creditor must ensure the borrower receives the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before consummation (closing) of the loan.
      • Changes Triggering a New Waiting Period: If the actual terms or costs change significantly prior to consummation, the creditor must issue a corrected Closing Disclosure, triggering a new three-business-day waiting period before closing can occur.
    • No Document-Preparation Fees Allowed: Under RESPA, lenders and settlement agents are strictly prohibited from charging a fee to prepare the Closing Disclosure, Loan Estimate, escrow account statement, or any other required TILA disclosure.
  • Closing in Escrow: Deposits, Exchange & Disbursement
    • How Escrow Protects Both Parties: A neutral third-party escrow agent / escrow holder (such as an attorney or representative of a title company, trust company, escrow company, or lender escrow department) holds all funds and documents until every contractual condition is met—ensuring the buyer's money is not released until the seller's title is acceptable.
      PartyRequired Escrow Deposits Prior to Closing
      Seller's Deposits• Executed deed conveying title to the buyer

      • Title evidence (abstract of title or title insurance commitment/policy)

      • Payoff letter from existing mortgagee (stating the exact payoff balance) or an estoppel certificate (stating the exact balance if the buyer is assuming the loan)

      • Affidavit as to debts and liens (sworn statement guaranteeing no undisclosed liens, unpaid repair/improvement bills, or title defects; required before the title insurer issues an owner's policy and gives the insurer the right to sue the seller if false)

      • Any other instruments required to clear title
      Buyer's Deposits• Balance of cash to close (typically via certified/cashier's check or wire)

      • Executed loan documents (if financing with a new loan)

      • Hazard insurance policy (including flood insurance, if required)

      • Property survey (if required by the purchase contract or lender)

      • Any other required transaction documents (such as the appraisal)
  • The Exchange & Post-Closing Disbursement:
    1. Once all conditions are satisfied and title is verified in the buyer's name, the closing agent collects all funds and completes the exchange.
    1. After closing, the closing agent issues all disbursement checks, prepares a final disbursement sheet confirming that total funds paid in equal total funds paid out, disburses net proceeds to the seller (after deducting seller closing costs and existing lien payoffs), and transmits the deed and mortgage/deed of trust to the county clerk for recording (ensuring the deed recording fee is paid).
  • Post-Closing Mortgage Escrow Accounts (Impound Accounts)
    • How It Works: After closing, the borrower makes monthly payments into an escrow account managed by their mortgage loan servicer as part of their regular monthly mortgage payment; the servicer holds those funds and pays the borrower's homeowners insurance premiums and property taxes when due.
    • When Required vs. Optional: Mandatory for many loans—including government-insured loans (FHA, VA) and certain high-LTV conventional loans—and optionally elected by other borrowers who prefer spreading large annual tax and insurance bills into predictable monthly installments.

Key Terms

  • Closing Disclosure - a form used to itemize services and fees charged to the borrower by the lender when applying for a real estate loan; aka closing statement
  • escrow - a process in which funds and/or financial documents are held by a disinterested third party on behalf of the other two parties in the real estate transaction until specific conditions are satisfied

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Closing
    • The consummation of the real estate transaction is known as the closing, or settlement. Closing is when title is officially transferred from seller to buyer in exchange for consideration (which is most likely to be money).
    • In many transactions, the closing is also when the buyer's mortgage funds are disbursed to cover the cost of the property. After closing, the purchase contract will have been fully executed.
  • Who's Who
    • Buyer: The buyer pays the seller and receives the title.
    • Seller: The seller receives payment and transfers title to the buyer.
    • Real estate agent (broker and/or sales agent): The agent accounts for all the transacted monies, fulfilling their fiduciary duty of accounting (so while you won't be preparing the settlement statement, for example, you will double-check that it's right).
    • Escrow officer: This is the person who runs the closing (also known as the closing agent).
    • Lender: The lender supplies the money for the purchase unless the buyer is buying in cash or using seller financing. A buyer contributes a down payment, and the lender provides the rest of the funds.
    • Title company agent: The title company agent does the title search on the property to discover any liens or title issues. They will then issue the title commitment and the title insurance (which, remember, serves as proof of marketable title). Title agents and escrow agents are two different people with two different jobs, though often they are both employed by the same larger company.
  • The Closing Disclosure
    • The Closing Disclosure (also called the closing statement) is the final itemization of all services and fees charged to the borrower by the lender when applying for a real estate loan.
    • For loans that require a Loan Estimate and that proceed to closing, creditors must provide the Closing Disclosure reflecting the actual terms of the transaction. The creditor is generally required to ensure that the consumer receives the Closing Disclosure no later than three business days before consummation of the loan.
