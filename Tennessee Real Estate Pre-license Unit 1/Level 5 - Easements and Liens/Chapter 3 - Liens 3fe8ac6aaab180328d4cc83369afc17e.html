Chapter 3 - Liens

Liens as Encumbrances

  • Definition: A legal or financial claim made by a creditor against real or personal property pledged by a debtor as collateral.
  • Encumbrance Classification: Functions as a financial encumbrance because it burdens the property title and impacts its marketability.
  • Foreclosure Rights: Provides the creditor the right to foreclose on or liquidate the property to recover unpaid debt if the debtor defaults (either immediately or via a legal lawsuit).
  • How Liens Affect Property Sales
    • Attachment to Property: Liens attach directly to the specific real estate parcel rather than to the individual homeowner.
    • Conveyance Requirements: A property burdened by liens cannot easily be transferred cleanly unless:
      • The seller satisfies and clears the lien on or before the closing date.
      • The buyer agrees to purchase the property subject to the existing lien (rare and unlikely).
    • Buyer Risk: Because liens attach to the property, any unreleased lien transfers with the title, making the new buyer responsible for the encumbrance.
  • Voluntary vs. Involuntary Liens
    • Voluntary Liens:
      • Definition: Created intentionally with the full consent and agreement of the property owner.
      • Mortgage Liens: The most common voluntary lien; established when a borrower pledges real estate as collateral to secure financing for a property purchase.
      • Home Equity Lines of Credit (HELOCs): A voluntary lien where a lender uses the owner's built-up property equity as collateral for a credit line.
    • Involuntary Liens:
      • Definition: Created automatically through operation of law without the property owner's consent or choice.
      • Examples: Real estate tax liens and court judgment liens.
  • General vs. Specific Liens
    • General Liens:
      • Scope: Claims against all real and personal property owned by the debtor.
      • Satisfaction: Allows both real estate and personal assets to be seized and liquidated to satisfy the underlying debt.
    • Specific Liens:
      • Scope: Claims attached only to a specific, designated parcel or item of property.
      • Satisfaction: Personal property and unattached real estate owned by the debtor are unaffected.
  • General Liens: Overview
    • Definition: Liens that attach to both real property and personal property owned by the debtor.
    • Primary Types:
      • Judgment liens
      • Estate and inheritance tax liens
      • Deceased persons' debt
      • IRS tax liens
  • Judgment Liens
    • Definition: Court decrees issued at the conclusion of lawsuits specifying that one party owes money to another, allowing the creditor to claim a lien against the debtor's assets.
    • Standard Classification: General, involuntary lien.
    • Financing Impact: Lenders typically deny new loan applications for debtors with active judgment liens, as the judgment lien takes priority over a new mortgage lien in foreclosure payouts.
    • Voluntary Exception (Confession of Judgment):
      • Occurs when a debtor voluntarily accepts a judgment via a written statement rather than through contested court proceedings.
      • Classified as a voluntary, general lien.
  • Judgment Liens in Tennessee (T.C.A. § 25-5-101)
    • Property Attachment: Attaches to real estate only (does not attach to personal property in TN).
    • Recording Requirements: Must be recorded at the county recording office where the debtor owns property; filing must specify judgment amount, civil action number, court name, and creation date.
    • Duration & Renewal: Valid for 10 years; requires a court order/extension to renew beyond the initial term.
  • Execution Liens (Writ of Execution)
    • Mechanism: A court order issued when an existing judgment or lien remains unsatisfied (unpaid).
    • Function: Authorizes court officers to seize and sell the debtor’s property at a public sale to pay off the debt.
    • Classification: Involuntary execution lien that attaches to the property until the court sale is complete.
  • Attachment Liens (Writ of Attachment)
    • Purpose: Prevents a defendant from selling or transferring property while a lawsuit is actively pending prior to final judgment.
    • Mechanism: A court order that seizes or encumbers property until the lawsuit reaches a verdict.
    • Plaintiff Requirement: The plaintiff must post a surety bond or cash deposit to protect the defendant from financial loss if the court rules in favor of the defendant.
    • Classification & Limits: General, involuntary lien attaching to all real and personal property owned by the debtor except protected homestead property.
  • Lis Pendens Notice: Definition & Function
    • Definition: A documented public filing recorded at the county courthouse giving formal notice that a lawsuit is actively pending against a specific piece of real estate.
    • Purpose: Protects third parties by establishing constructive notice of a potential claim on title during the gap between the initial filing of a lawsuit and the final court judgment.
  • Impact on Real Estate Buyers & Title Search
    • Buyer Warning: Serves as a major red flag notifying prospective buyers of potential litigation, clouds on title, or ownership disputes affecting the property.
    • Discovery via Title Search: Uncovered during the due diligence process when a title company conducts a title search and compiles the abstract of title.
    • Transaction Outcome: Prompts buyers to evaluate the legal risk associated with the property; in most cases, a lis pendens causes buyers to terminate or back out of the transaction.
  • General Tax Liens & Statutory Liens
    • Statutory Lien Definition: A lien created automatically by operation of law or statute (rather than by contract or common law).
    • Tax Lien Trigger: Imposed against a debtor's property when they become delinquent in paying legally mandated taxes.
    • General vs. Specific Distinction:
      • General Tax Liens: Attach to all real estate and personal property owned by the taxpayer (e.g., federal income taxes, estate taxes).
      • Specific Tax Liens: Attach only to a specific parcel of real property (e.g., real estate ad valorem property taxes).
  • Estate & Inheritance Tax Liens
    • Estate Taxes: Applied to the net assets left behind upon a person's death after deducting qualifying administrative expenses (such as funeral costs).
    • Classification: Functions as an involuntary, general lien that burdens all real and personal assets within the estate until taxes are paid.
  • Federal Tax Liens (IRS)
    • Trigger: Incurred when a taxpayer fails or refuses to pay assessed federal taxes, such as federal income tax, to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS).
    • Classification: A general, involuntary lien.
    • Scope of Attachment: Attaches to all real estate and personal property owned by the defaulting taxpayer, as well as property acquired after the lien is established.
  • Specific Liens: Overview
    • Definition: Claims that attach strictly to one specific, designated parcel of real estate (or item of property) rather than all assets owned by a debtor.
    • Primary Types:
      • Mortgage lien
      • Mechanic's lien
      • Vendor's lien
      • Vendee's lien
      • Bail bond lien
      • Municipal utility lien
  • Mortgage Liens
    • Classification: Voluntary, specific lien.
    • Mechanism: Established with the homebuyer's express consent; the property being purchased serves as pledged collateral to secure the loan.
    • Scope of Attachment: Attaches strictly to the specific real estate parcel pledged for the debt (other real estate or personal assets remain unaffected).
    • Enforcement Rights: Grants the lender the legal authority to foreclose and force the sale of the property to recover unpaid balances if the borrower defaults.
    • Lien Priority: Generally holds priority over all other recorded encumbrances and liens, except superior real estate property tax liens.
  • Mechanic's Liens
    • Classification: Specific, involuntary lien.
    • Protected Parties: Contractors, subcontractors, material suppliers, architects, engineers, and surveyors who provide labor, services, or materials that enhance property value.
    • Trigger: Property owner fails to pay for authorized work or materials.
    • Enforcement: Enforced through legal action that can result in a court-ordered sale of the property (excluding protected homesteads) to satisfy the debt.
  • Vendor's Liens
    • Classification: Specific, involuntary (statutory) lien.
    • Trigger: Created when a buyer does not pay the full purchase price to the seller (seller financing), using the property as collateral for the remaining balance.
    • Enforcement: Sellers cannot execute a direct foreclosure like a mortgage lender; they must file a lawsuit to force a judicial sale of the property.
    • Legal Context: Commonly utilized in transactions involving warranty deeds.
  • Vendee's Liens
    • Classification: Specific, equitable lien.
    • Trigger: Protects a buyer (vendee) who fulfills their contractual obligations (e.g., under a contract for deed) when the seller defaults or fails to convey title/deed as agreed.
  • Bail Bond Liens
    • Classification: Specific, voluntary lien.
    • Trigger: Established when a property owner pledges real estate as collateral for a bail bond in lieu of cash after being charged with a crime.
    • Enforcement: Enforceable by a court officer or sheriff if the accused fails to make required court appearances.
  • Municipal Utility Liens
    • Classification: Specific, involuntary lien.
    • Trigger: Imposed by a local municipality against a property when the owner becomes delinquent on municipal utility bills.
  • Mechanic's & Materialman's Liens in Tennessee: Core Concepts
    • Terminology: Tennessee law groups work and materials together under mechanic's lien rules:
      • Mechanic's Lien: Applies to unpaid labor or professional work performed.
      • Materialman's Lien: Applies to unpaid materials or equipment supplied.
  • Eligible Claimants (Lien Rights by Property Type)
    • Commercial / Non-Owner-Occupied / >4-Unit Residential: Expansive lien rights; includes prime contractors, subcontractors, laborers, equipment and material suppliers at any tier, surveyors, architects, and engineers.
    • 1- to 4-Unit Owner-Occupied Residential: Lien rights are restricted strictly to the prime contractor (or those contracting directly with the owner).
    • Owner Acting as Prime Contractor: If an owner acts as their own prime contractor on a single-family home, laborers and suppliers contracting directly with the owner/prime contractor also retain lien rights.
  • Prohibition of Lien Waivers (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-11-124(b))
    • Statutory Protection: Tennessee explicitly prohibits the contractual waiver of lien rights. Any contract provision attempting to waive mechanic's lien rights is void and unenforceable as against public policy.
  • Homeowner Right of Rejection ("Escape Clause")
    • Cancellation Window: Homeowners have 3 days after receiving notice of the right to lien to reject the contractor’s lien right and cancel the contract.
    • Waiver of Rejection: If the homeowner does not exercise cancellation within 3 days, the right of rejection is waived.
  • Lien Attachment & Priority
    • Proactive Attachment: Attaches at the visible commencement of work (rather than when payment becomes delinquent) and should be incorporated into the owner-contractor agreement.
    • Priority Impact: If work begins prior to the recording or funding of a new mortgage or HELOC, the mechanic's lien maintains superior priority over that loan in a foreclosure.
  • Notice of Nonpayment (Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-11-145)
    • Service Requirements: Must be officially served on both the property owner and the prime contractor.
    • Rolling Deadline: Must be served within 90 days from the last day of each month in which labor or materials were provided.
    • Multi-Month Rule: Projects spanning multiple months require separate notices for each month work/materials were supplied, rather than a single notice at project completion.
  • Filing a Mechanic's Lien in Tennessee
    • Standard Filing Deadline: Must be recorded in the county register's office where the work occurred within 90 days of full project completion or abandonment.
    • Notice of Completion Exception: If the property owner files an official Notice of Completion, the filing window accelerates to 30 days from the date that notice was recorded.
    • Forfeiture of Rights: Failing to record the lien within the designated statutory period (90 days standard / 30 days with Notice of Completion) permanently terminates all mechanic's lien rights.
    • Direct Lien Duration: Contractors, laborers, or suppliers holding a direct contract with the property owner retain a direct lien on the real estate valid for 1 year following project completion.
    • Notice of Lien Recording: Direct claimants must record a Notice of Lien in the county register's office within 90 days of project completion to establish legal priority against subsequent buyers or encumbrances without notice.
  • Enforcing the Lien
    • Action Window: The claimant must initiate formal legal enforcement (foreclosure proceedings) within 90 days of filing the lien.
    • Unenforceability: Failing to commence a lawsuit within this 90-day enforcement window renders the recorded lien legally void and unenforceable.
  • Releasing the Lien
    • Claimant Obligation: Upon full receipt of payment and debt satisfaction, the claimant must issue an official lien release within 30 days.
    • Owner Recording & Penalties: The property owner records the release to clear title defects; claimants who fail to provide a timely release face statutory financial penalties.
  • Lien Priority & Relation-Back Rule
    • General Rule: Liens are typically prioritized and satisfied in chronological order based on creation or recording dates.
    • Relation-Back Principle: Tennessee mechanic's liens date back to the day work visible commenced on the property rather than when payment became delinquent.
    • Impact on Mortgages: A mechanic's lien takes superior priority over a mortgage or HELOC if the contractor started physical work on the site before the loan was funded or recorded.
  • Specific Tax Liens: Overview
    • Definition: Tax claims that attach exclusively to a single, designated parcel of real estate rather than extending to all of a taxpayer's personal and real property.
    • Legal Basis: Imposed because the underlying tax obligation is assessed directly against the physical real estate itself.
    • Government Purpose: Levied by state, county, and municipal governments to raise revenue for local public services, community operations, and infrastructure projects.
  • Primary Types of Real Estate Property Taxes
    • Ad Valorem Taxes: General real estate property taxes calculated as a percentage of the property's assessed value ("according to value").
    • Special Assessments: Targeted taxes or fees levied against specific real estate parcels to pay for localized public improvements that directly enhance or benefit those properties.
  • Ad Valorem Taxes (Property Taxes)
    • Definition & Basis: Latin for "according to value"; property taxes levied on real estate based directly on its assessed value.
    • Levying Entities: Authorized governmental bodies including counties, municipalities/cities, towns, park districts, hospital districts, water districts, and school districts.
    • Lien Classification: Functions as a specific, involuntary, statutory lien created by law to fund local agencies and public operations.
  • Consequences of Delinquent Property Taxes
    • Escalation Process:
      1. Issuance of official delinquency notices by the taxing authority.
      1. Placement of a statutory property tax lien on the specific real estate parcel.
      1. Foreclosure and forced sale of the property as a final recourse to satisfy unpaid tax balances.
  • Property Tax Liens vs. IRS Tax Liens
    • Property Tax Liens: Calculated from individual parcel value; attaches strictly to that single real estate property (specific lien).
    • IRS Tax Liens: Calculated from personal income tax delinquencies; attaches to all real and personal property owned by the individual (general lien).
  • Special Assessments: Core Concept & Purpose
    • Definition: Specific taxes levied against real estate properties that receive direct benefits from public infrastructure or local improvement projects.
    • Special Assessment Districts: Defined geographical boundaries established to encompass the specific properties benefiting from and funding local improvements.
    • Lien & Legal Classification: Functions as a statutory, specific lien attached directly to the benefited real estate until paid in full; nonpayment empowers the governing municipality or district to place a tax lien on the property.
  • Voluntary vs. Involuntary Special Assessments
    • Involuntary Special Assessments: Mandated by local government entities without homeowner consent (e.g., city-directed curb and street upgrades across an entire neighborhood).
    • Voluntary Special Assessments: Requested by property owners who formally petition the local municipality for a specific public improvement project (e.g., adding neighborhood sidewalks).
  • Common Projects Funded by Special Assessments
    • Street paving and resurfacing
    • Sidewalk installation and repairs
    • Curb and gutter construction
    • Installation of street lights and utility extensions
  • Cost-Allocation Methods
    • Equal Basis: Total project expenses are divided equally among all impacted property owners (e.g., splitting the installation cost of shared street lighting).
    • Prorated Basis: Costs are distributed proportionally based on specific property dimensions (e.g., charging sidewalk improvements per front-foot of lot boundary).
  • Lien Priority: Core Concept & Purpose
    • Definition: The legal order in which multiple liens recorded against a property must be satisfied/settled in the event of foreclosure or property sale.
    • Financial Significance: In a foreclosure, the property is auctioned off and proceeds are distributed down the priority chain. Lienholders lower in priority risk receiving no payment if the property's equity is exhausted by higher-priority debts.
  • General Rule vs. Tax Exception ("First Position")
    • General Rule ("First in Time is First in Line"): Liens are prioritized chronologically based on the order and date they were recorded in county public records.
    • Super-Priority Exception: Government tax liens—specifically ad valorem real estate property taxes and special assessments—automatically jump to the front of the line, taking precedence over all other recorded liens (occupying "first position").
  • Senior vs. Junior Liens
    • Senior (Superior) Lien: A lien that holds higher priority over other claims (e.g., ad valorem property tax liens or a primary purchase-money mortgage).
    • Junior (Inferior) Lien: A lien that is subordinate to higher-priority liens.
    • Interest Rate Impact: Junior liens (such as 2nd or 3rd mortgages/HELOCs) carry higher interest rates to compensate lenders for the elevated risk of non-payment during foreclosure.
  • Subordination Agreements
    • Definition: A written contract separate from the sales contract in which an existing lienholder voluntarily agrees to alter lien priority, allowing a newly recorded lien to take precedence over their existing claim.
    • Lender Requirement: Lenders providing primary purchase or refinance financing generally require first-position lien status before funding a loan.
    • Common Usage: Typically needed when refinancing a primary mortgage on a home that already has an active secondary mortgage or HELOC.
  • Scenario Breakdown: Homeowner Mike
    • Setup:
      1. Mike buys a house using a primary mortgage (1st position).
      1. Mike takes out a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) to remodel his basement (2nd position).
    • Refinancing Conflict: Mike attempts to refinance his primary loan. Paying off the original primary loan extinguishes the 1st position claim, making the new refinanced mortgage junior to the existing HELOC unless action is taken.
    • Resolution: The primary lender requires the HELOC lender to execute a subordination agreement, voluntarily agreeing to keep the HELOC in 2nd position behind the newly refinanced mortgage loan. If the HELOC lender refuses, the refinance cannot proceed unless Mike pays off the HELOC in full.
  • Termination and Removal of Liens: Overview
    • Conveyance with Title: Unsatisfied liens remain attached to the real estate upon transfer; the lien is not nullified by a title transfer and becomes the obligation/encumbrance of the new owner.
    • Pre-Closing Resolution: In standard transactions, sellers clear outstanding liens prior to or at settlement to ensure the conveyance of a clean, marketable title.
  • Methods of Clearing & Terminating Liens
    • Transferring General Liens:
      • Because general liens are not tied exclusively to a single real estate parcel, a seller can negotiate with the creditor to release the lien from the subject property and transfer/re-attach it to another acceptable property or alternative collateral owned by the seller.
    • Payment at Closing (Sale Proceeds):
      • Sufficient Proceeds: Settlement agents withhold the required payoff amount from the sale proceeds at closing to satisfy the creditor in full.
      • Satisfaction Document: Following payment, an official satisfaction of lien (or release of lien) is executed and recorded in the county public records to formally extinguish the encumbrance.
    • Payment at Closing (Seller Shortfall):
      • If total debt exceeds sale proceeds, the seller must bring out-of-pocket funds to the closing table to satisfy the remaining debt balance and secure the lien release.
  • Scenario Breakdown: Foreclosure & Lien Priority ("Foreclosing on Gene")
    • Lien Creation Timeline:
      • 2015 (1st Lien): Primary purchase mortgage secured.
      • 2017 (2nd Lien): Home equity loan taken out for pool installation.
      • 2019 (3rd Lien): Third lien secured to replace the HVAC system.
    • Financial Default & Foreclosure Sale:
      • Foreclosure Sale Proceeds: Property sold at courthouse auction for $52,500 cash.
      • Outstanding Debts:
        • 1st Mortgage Balance (including foreclosure fees): $47,000.
        • 2nd Mortgage Balance (pool loan): $9,000.
        • 3rd Mortgage Balance (HVAC loan): $1,500.
    • Distribution of Foreclosure Proceeds ("First in Time, First in Line"):
      • 1st Position (1st Mortgage): Paid $47,000 in full.
      • Remaining Funds: $52,500 - $47,000 = $5,500.
      • 2nd Position (Pool Loan): Receives the remaining $5,500 (leaving a $3,500 uncollected deficiency).
      • 3rd Position (HVAC Loan): Receives $0 (proceeds completely exhausted).
    • Risk & Interest Rate Correlation:
      • Lower-priority (junior) liens face substantially higher financial risk of unpaid balances upon foreclosure.
      • Lenders charge higher interest rates on secondary and tertiary loans to offset this risk.

Key Terms

  • general lien - a lien for which the real estate AND personal property may be sold to satisfy the debt
  • junior lien - a lien that has another (senior) lien superseding it in priority; aka inferior lien
  • lien priority - the order in which liens against a property will be settled
  • lis pendens - a document recording at the courthouse giving notice that a lawsuit is pending on a particular piece of property
  • mechanic's lien - a type of involuntary, specific lien that a mechanic or materialman can impose upon a property if the property owner fails to pay for materials or work done on the property
  • senior lien - a lien that comes first on the priority of liens, often a tax lien; aka superior lien
  • special assessment lien - a lien placed against property to collect taxes for a specific improvement benefiting the property
  • specific lien - a lien that applies to a certain property only
  • subordination agreement - a contract that gives a mortgage recorded at a later date priority over a previously recorded mortgage

Key Concepts & Principles

  • A voluntary lien is a lien that is created on purpose and with the agreement of the owner of the property in question.
  • An involuntary lien is obtained through an action of law without the owner's consent.
  • A general lien is a lien for which the real estate AND personal property may be sold to satisfy the debt.
  • A specific lien is a lien that applies to a certain property only. Personal property is not affected.
  • Lien Priority: Principles & Rules
    • General Rule ("First in Time is First in Line"): Liens are generally prioritized and satisfied in chronological order based on the exact date and time they were officially recorded in public records.
    • Super-Priority Exception ("First Position"): Government real estate tax liens—specifically ad valorem property taxes and special assessments—automatically take priority over all other recorded liens, regardless of when those other liens were recorded.
  • Senior vs. Junior Liens
    • Senior (Superior) Lien: Any lien that holds a higher priority position in the legal payout order over other encumbrances (e.g., property tax liens or primary purchase mortgages).
    • Junior (Inferior) Lien: Any lien that is superseded by a higher-priority (senior) lien in the foreclosure payout hierarchy.
