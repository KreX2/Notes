Chapter 4 - Responsibilities and Scenarios

Affiliate Broker (Agent) Fair Housing Best Practices

  • Search Strictly by Price Range & Objective Criteria: Select properties across all neighborhoods within the buyer's budget and stated criteria—never filter or steer neighborhoods based on where you assume a client would feel comfortable, even with good intentions.
  • Use Inclusive Marketing Language: Focus property descriptions on physical features of the home and neighborhood rather than the "ideal buyer" (avoid phrases like "perfect for a young couple" or "great for empty nesters", which imply exclusion of families with children or other protected groups).
  • Use a Standardized Intake Questionnaire: Ask every prospect the exact same needs-and-wants questions (e.g., preferred square footage, number of bedrooms, architectural style, or accessibility requirements) and keep consistent written notes.
  • Verify Exemptions With Legal Counsel: Fair housing exemptions are narrow and technical—consult your principal broker or a real estate attorney before assuming any property or client is exempt.
  • Practice Cultural Awareness & Accountable Apologies: Respect differences in communication style, personal space, and negotiation norms. If a client says a remark was offensive:
    1. Listen without interrupting or getting defensive.
    1. Express gratitude for bringing it up.
    1. Apologize sincerely and move on without centering yourself, listing credentials, or asking the client to reassure you.
  • Principal Broker Compliance, Supervision & Vicarious Liability
    • Core Brokerage Compliance Duties:
      • Master Federal, State & Local Rules: Stay current on Fair Housing Act, Tennessee Human Rights Act (THRA), and local municipal ordinances.
      • Community Engagement & Agent Training: Partner with local fair housing organizations and ensure all affiliate brokers complete mandatory fair housing education and office training.
      • Documentation & Recordkeeping: Maintain complete transaction files, written intake logs, showing records, and email trails to defend against false claims or substantiate complaints.
      • Display the HUD Fair Housing Poster (24 C.F.R. Part 110): Prominently display the official 11" x 14" Equal Housing Opportunity poster in all brokerage places of business (failure to display it constitutes prima facie evidence of a discriminatory housing practice).
      • ADA Compliance (Title III): Ensure the brokerage office and client services are accessible to individuals with disabilities, and advise commercial clients to obtain professional ADA accessibility audits.
    • Vicarious Liability Standard (Meyer v. Holley, 2003):
      • Under traditional agency principles, the brokerage entity/corporation is vicariously liable for discriminatory acts committed by its agents within the scope of their agency, whereas individual owners, officers, or designated brokers are not automatically personally liable without personal fault.
      • However, a principal broker or owner can be held personally liable if they directly participated in, authorized, failed to supervise/train against, or tolerated a discriminatory environment.
    • Fair Housing Testing (Undercover Testers):
      • HUD and private fair housing organizations routinely deploy undercover testers—individuals posing as prospective buyers or renters with no bona fide intent to transact—to compare how protected-class and non-protected-class prospects are treated.
      • If testers find no violation, the brokerage is never notified; if an infraction is uncovered, the broker is notified and a formal investigation begins.
  • Property Manager Dos & Assistance Animal Rules
    • Respect Non-English & ESL Speakers: Apply identical rental qualification criteria regardless of primary language; never deny or restrict units because an applicant speaks English as a second language.
    • Maintain Written, Transparent Tenant Screening Criteria: Provide objective qualification standards upfront and give rejected applicants clear, accurate reasons for denial upon request.
    • Avoid Building/Floor Steering & Focus Rules on Conduct: Never steer families with children or minorities to specific buildings or ground floors. Phrase community rules around neutral behavior ("No running or loud noise in the laundry room") rather than targeting protected groups ("No children allowed in the laundry room").
    • Grant Reasonable Accommodations (High-Risk Violation Zone): Never summarily reject an accommodation request (e.g., reserved accessible parking, communication aids, or assistance animals) without careful Fair Housing Act review, especially when a disability is non-obvious.

  • Handling Discriminatory Sellers & Buyers
    • Discriminatory Sellers:
      • Set Ground Rules Upfront: Before signing a listing agreement, obtain the seller's acknowledgment that you will comply strictly with fair housing laws, reject any protected-class criteria in evaluating offers, terminate the listing if the seller attempts to discriminate, and report violations to your principal broker.
      • Red Flags of Seller Discrimination: Refusing a full-price offer from a protected-class buyer, abruptly pulling the property off the market to avoid a protected-class buyer, or accepting a lower offer over a higher offer submitted by a protected-class buyer.
    • Discriminatory Buyers:
      • Never Answer Demographic Steering Questions: If a buyer asks about the racial, religious, or ethnic makeup of a neighborhood, never speculate or provide demographic breakdowns—doing so constitutes unlawful steering.
      • Use a Prepared Brokerage Policy Script: Respond neutrally and consistently, such as: "It is our company's policy and legal obligation not to provide demographic information about neighborhoods. You are welcome to research public data sources independently."
    • Shared Liability & Duty to Disengage:
      • Never go along with a client's discriminatory remarks or instructions—an agent who complies with or facilitates a client's discrimination is personally and equally liable under fair housing laws.
      • Speak up immediately, document the incident in detail, notify your principal broker, and terminate the agency relationship if the client refuses to comply.
  • Coded Language ("Fitting In") & National Association of REALTORS® (NAR) Fair Housing Standards
    • Handling Coded Client Preferences ("I know what you mean"):
      • Even when a seller avoids overt slurs and uses coded phrases like only wanting buyers who "fit in", agreeing or nodding along puts the licensee in dangerous legal territory.
      • Required Action: Clarify immediately that all bona fide offers will be presented and evaluated strictly on objective financial terms without regard to protected characteristics, document the conversation in writing, and report the incident to your principal broker.
    • NAR Code of Ethics (Article 10) & Equal Professional Service Model:
      • Protected Classes Under Article 10: Mirrors the federal Fair Housing Act (race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin) and explicitly includes sexual orientation and gender identity, plus Standard of Practice 10-5 prohibiting harassing or hate speech in real estate activities.
      • Equal Professional Service Model: A systematic workflow ensuring every customer and client receives identical, unbiased service through:
        • Standardized initial intake and needs-and-wants questions
        • Consistent written recordkeeping for all prospects
        • Objective fact-gathering rather than assumptions
        • Letting consumers set their own price, neighborhood, and property limits
        • Offering a wide variety of housing choices across areas
  • Fair Housing Complaint Data & Ongoing Disparities (HUD / NFHA Reports)
    • Breakdown of Reported Fair Housing Complaints (2024 Fair Housing Trends Data):
      • Disability: 52.6% (52.61%) of all complaints—consistently the largest category
      • Race: 17% (16%–17% range across recent reporting cycles)
      • Sex: 7.5%
      • Familial status: 6.3%
      • Regulators estimate roughly 4 million instances of rental housing discrimination occur each year—far exceeding formal complaints filed.
    • HUD Paired-Testing Housing Discrimination Study Findings (Compared to White Prospects):
      • Black prospects: Told about 11.4% fewer rental units and shown 4.2% fewer units; told about 17% fewer homes for sale and shown 17.7% fewer homes.
      • Hispanic prospects: Told about 12.5% fewer rental units and shown 7.5% fewer units (while home-purchase showings showed no statistically significant gap).
      • Asian prospects: Told about 9.8% fewer rental units and shown 6.6% fewer units; told about 15.5% fewer homes for sale and shown 18.8% fewer homes.
  • Fair Housing Advertising Guidelines (42 U.S.C. § 3604(c))
    • Broad Scope of "Advertising": Covers print ads, online listings, MLS remarks, yard signs, brochures, rental applications, TV/radio spots, social media posts, and spoken statements.
    • Golden Rule of Advertising: "Describe the property, not the people." Highlight physical amenities, floor plans, proximity to landmarks, and neutral facts—never describe the type of buyer or tenant who belongs there.

Key Concepts & Principles

  • Affiliate Broker Dos
    • Stick to price range
    • Be inclusive
    • Ask standard questions
    • Get help with exemptions
    • Be sensitive to cultural differences
  • Principal Broker Dos
    • Know the rules
    • Get into the community
    • Educate affiliate brokers
    • Keep good records
    • Put up fair housing poster
    • Comply with the ADA
    • Understand liability
  • Property Manager Dos
    • Respect ESL speakers
    • Be transparent
    • Avoid steering
    • Avoid familial status discrimination
    • Make reasonable accommodations
  • Discriminatory Clients
    • If you're in a situation where a client asks a question like, “What is the racial makeup of this neighborhood?” you do not have to answer. Many brokers will have a prepared response for their sales agents to recite in these kinds of situations, such as:
    • "It is the policy of our company not to answer questions of that nature. If you are concerned, you are free to do some research on your own."
    • By responding in this way, the agent has clearly let the clients know that there are some topics that will not be discussed by the agent.
    • Indeed, if an agent goes along with a client's discriminatory act or speech, the agent is just as liable for violating fair housing laws.
  • Advertising
    • The Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) offers several guidelines around advertising. HUD outlaws the really obvious, discriminatory stuff. The more subtle stuff ("facially neutral") is not in itself a violation.
